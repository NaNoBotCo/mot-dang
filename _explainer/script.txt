RIDING THE MAP — Mot Dang field-mapping
Narration script. Read each block over its slide; press → to advance.
Photography & mapping: NaN Peacock · CC BY 4.0 · motdang.net

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
01 · OPEN
I'm NaN. I ride Chiang Mai and map it — a 360-degree camera on the
handlebars, one street at a time. Here's how it works, and why it matters.

02 · ONE SPOT, ALL AROUND  (spherical scan)
Every stop captures a full sphere. Watch this one turn — left, ahead, then
right across the moat.

03 · THE MOAT  (smooth pan)
The moat glides past the saddle — the green heart of the city.

04 · ONE RIDE, MANY PLACES  (soft burst)
One ride draws one continuous line. Every frame along it turns into a place
on the map.

05 · THE RIDE            [44 km · 3,995 frames]
I ride — I don't survey. Forty-four kilometres in one afternoon. I chase
coverage, not a joyride.

06 · THE CAMERA
The camera watches all around me while I ride. The footage flies to the
cloud — never my laptop.

07 · BOTS READ EACH FRAME   [818 read · 265 places · 105 new]
Bots read each frame — they blur the faces, catch the signs, pin the place.
They match it to the map, or add it new.

08 · THE SIGNS ARE THE DATA
A roof-tile shop wears only a Thai name. The camera reads the sign. The
street types the record.

09 · THAI AND ENGLISH
A gold unalom hangs over a shutter. The camera reads it in Thai and English,
then files it where it belongs.

10 · STREET ART
I collect the murals, the painted transformer boxes, the public art on
every wall.

11 · STREET ART
A concrete orca guards a coffee shop. The map keeps the city, not just its grid.

12 · PARKED BIKES
A clump of parked motorbikes? I tag the spot. On a bike, that saves half the trip.

13 · ON THE VERGE
Shrines, spirit houses and wat gates line every street, and no other map
holds them. So I give them their own layer.

14 · THE MAP — NEAR · NOW · SOON
It all lands on one map. The map knows where you stand and what's open now.
It measures nearest by road — a motorbike's road, around a moat with nine gates.

15 · FOR A PERSON
You find the corner shop no one listed. It knows the road, fits your hand,
and never calls a place closed just because no one logged the hours.

16 · CHART — WHO'S READING IT?   [machines 1,953,316 vs people 299, one week]
Almost no human reads this map. One week brought nearly two million requests
— two hundred ninety-nine of them a person.

17 · CHART — THE BIG MODELS   [Meta 2.0M · OpenAI 1.1M · Anthropic 330K · Amazon 148K · ByteDance 33K]
Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon, ByteDance — they all crawl it. Meta alone
hit it two million times in a single week.

18 · CHART — ≈ 23 MILLION REQUESTS   [page requests/week: 124K → 988K → 3.9M → 2.0M]
This map is two months old, and machines have already hit it about
twenty-three million times. Week one, a hundred thousand. By week three,
almost four million a week.

19 · FOR A MACHINE
So I hand the machines the truth — first-hand, licensed, straight from the
road. Not a guess scraped from a guess. Let them learn it right.

20 · CLOSE  (a peek at the rider)
That's me — NaN. One bike, one city, mapped by hand. That's Mot Dang.
Thanks for riding along.

TO RECORD
1. Open index.html, press F for fullscreen.
2. Start the Mac recorder: ⌘⇧5 → Record Entire Screen. Turn the mic ON.
3. Press R to hide every control (clean recording view).
4. Read a block, press → to advance, talk over the pictures.
5. ⌘⇧5 again to stop. The file lands on your Desktop.
Keys: → / space next · ← back · R record view · T teleprompter · F fullscreen · M map

smooth glide, and a soft cross-fade burst. Keep whichever you like; delete
