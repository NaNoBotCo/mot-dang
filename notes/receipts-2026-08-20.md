# The receipts pack — link health as the thing other people cite

Drafted 2026-08-20 for Nan's review. **Nothing here has been posted or sent.**
Posting and mailing are hers; these are drafts to edit or bin.

Menu item 1 of the SEO-dogfood session. The aim is not traffic — it is
inbound links, because Search Console shows 5,964 pages sitting in
"discovered, not indexed", and that queue moves on authority rather than on
more pages.

## Why this study and not another

It is the only thing Mot Dang holds that a stranger cannot get anywhere else:
995 official-website links opened by hand, one at a time, with the verdicts
published as raw data and the checker published as source. Google will not
sell that. Nobody has measured it for a Thai city. And the audience most
likely to care — people who do local SEO for a living — is exactly the
audience that links to things.

**What shipped alongside these drafts** (in the build, not deployed by me):

- `/reach.html` now has a bilingual title and description. It was
  `ลิงก์ที่ยังเปิดได้ · มดแดง` with a Thai-only description — the flagship
  study, illegible to the people meant to cite it.
- It has its own share card, `assets/og/reach.png`: 995 dots, one per link,
  in the page's own three colours. It was unfurling as the generic ant.
- `tests/test_reach_card.py` holds the card and the page to one definition of
  "broken", one palette, and one dot per link.

## The numbers — recrawled 2026-08-21, now complete coverage

Nan gave the go-ahead and `check_links.py` checked the 204 links that had
never been checked (995 distinct links from 1,379 places; 791 came from cache).
**Every outbound official-site link in the directory now carries a dated
verdict. Coverage is 100%.**

**228** links filed as "website" are really Facebook or Instagram pages. Of the
**767** that are genuinely websites, **325 (42%) no longer answer**:

| verdict | n | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| dns | 198 | the domain itself is gone |
| gone | 33 | 404 |
| http-error | 33 | returns an error |
| tls | 15 | invalid certificate |
| timeout | 14 | never answers |
| empty | 9 | comes back blank |
| parked | 7 | parked or for sale |
| server-error | 7 | server error |
| error | 7 | malformed link |
| down | 2 | refuses connections |

**440 answer.** Wayback has a copy for **272** of the broken ones.

**The headline moved from 47% to 42%, and the reason is worth stating if
anyone asks:** the first run covered 794 of the links we then held; this one
covers all 995. The newly-checked ones skew healthier — they are newer
records, added more recently, so their sites have had less time to lapse. The
web did not heal. Coverage grew.

Small wrinkle, 2 links wide: a site that now forwards to a social page
(`moved-social`) is counted as a working site by both the page and the card.
Arguably it is not a website any more. It moves nothing — 42% either way — but
it is the kind of thing a careful reader may notice, so do not be surprised by
it.

## A fork worth deciding before you post

The dead-domain list is, read a certain way, a prospecting list: 198 Chiang
Mai and Chiang Rai businesses whose domains lapsed are 198 sales
conversations for a web person. Handing that to the farang SEO crowd as a
lead list would earn links fast — it is the single most useful thing they
could receive.

It also turns your neighbours into somebody else's leads, brokered by you,
which sits badly beside a site whose whole posture is that the shop owner
holds the information and corrects it free.

**These drafts take the other road:** they publish the method and the dataset
and let readers notice the implication themselves. The link is earned either
way; the difference is who is doing the brokering. Say the word if you want
the blunter version — it is a two-line edit, not a rewrite.

## Post 1 — r/SEO, r/bigseo, r/webdev (the main one)

> **I opened every business website link in a Thai city directory. 42% of the
> real ones are dead.**
>
> I maintain an open directory for Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai — about 18,000
> places, built from OpenStreetMap plus field work. Every place record can
> carry an official website, so I opened all 995 of them, one at a
> time, and wrote down what actually happened.
>
> First surprise: **228 of the 995 were not websites at all.** They were
> Facebook or Instagram pages filed in the website field. That is just under a
> quarter of everything labelled "official site".
>
> Of the 767 that were genuinely websites, **325 — 42% — no longer answer.**
> The failure modes matter more than the headline: only 33 were 404s. **198
> were DNS failures**, meaning the domain itself is gone. Another 15 had
> expired certificates, 7 were parked or for sale, 14 never responded at all.
>
> This is not a story about bad webmasters. It is the ordinary shape of the
> small-business web here: someone built a site a decade ago, the actual
> conversation moved to LINE and Facebook, and the domain lapsed quietly. The
> business is fine. The link is not.
>
> What I did about it, in case it is useful to anyone building a directory:
>
> - Contact channels are now **ranked by what actually gets an answer** —
>   phone, then LINE, then Facebook, then a verified website. A dead site is
>   named and dated but never hyperlinked; the Wayback copy is linked instead
>   (272 of them have one).
> - A "website" that is really a Facebook URL is normalised into the Facebook
>   channel rather than filed as a website.
> - `sameAs` in the structured data carries only verified-live channels. It
>   was publishing dead URLs, which is worse than publishing nothing.
>
> Method, raw verdicts and the checker are all open — no signup, no API key:
>
> - Findings: https://motdang.net/reach.html
> - Every verdict with its date and Wayback snapshot: https://motdang.net/data/linkhealth.json
> - The checker: https://motdang.net/source/check_links.py
>
> It follows redirects by hand to catch offsite and parked landings, verifies
> certificates, and classifies walled gardens as unverifiable rather than
> guessing. Every one of the 995 links the directory holds, checked 2026-08-21.
>
> Happy to run the same check against another city's dataset if anyone wants
> a comparison — I have no idea whether 42% is high.

**Note on that last line:** it is a real offer and it is the sentence most
likely to start a conversation with the people you want. It also invites work.
Cut it if you would rather not.
