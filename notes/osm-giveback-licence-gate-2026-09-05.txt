────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE HEADLINE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Broad is the right call and it cannot be executed on the current schema.

Provenance in this corpus is RECORD-level: `sources[]` says where the record
came from, never where a FIELD came from.  OSM asks the opposite question —
"what is the source of this tag" — one field at a time.  For a record with
an OSM source and a second source, we cannot prove which of the two gave the
phone number, so we cannot honestly answer OSM's question about it.

That is not a reason to stop.  It is the reason the giveable set is 517
records today rather than 6,667, and it names the fix: per-field provenance,
which is the same schema work as the `offers[]` provenance in Phase 3 of the
cure plan.  One change, two payoffs, again.

C  THE LICENCE, READ — 6,150 records (4,223 pinned)          MUCH BETTER
   NEWS THAN THIS NOTE FIRST SAID.  I downloaded and read the actual text:
   "Terms and Conditions for DGA Open Government License", Version 1.0,
   10 June 2021 (data.go.th → /pages/dga-open-government-license → the EN
   PDF).  Saved reasoning below; the numbers first.

   FIRST FINDING — IT IS ONE LICENCE, NOT FIVE.  Every register in tier C
   except OBEC carries a `ref` of https://data.go.th/dataset/… :
       2,084  ONAB ทะเบียนวัด        data.go.th/dataset/onab_temples
       1,660  data.go.th (assorted)  incl. อย. drug-location, กรมธนารักษ์
         480  CITIZENinfo (DGA)      data.go.th/dataset/00170665-…
         168  สช. private schools    data.go.th/dataset/gdpublish-cer
       1,077  OBEC สพฐ.              gdcatalog.go.th — whose dataset page
                                     links back to a data.go.th dataset
   So 5,073 records ride on ONE licence text, and the last 1,077 ride on a
   sibling platform that points at the same place.  The four "different
   licence strings" in our own metadata are what each DATASET's licence
   field happened to say; the PLATFORM's terms are the floor under all of
   them, because the Terms state that a government organization accepts
   them at the moment it publishes a dataset there.

   SECOND FINDING — WHAT THE TEXT ACTUALLY GRANTS.  Under "Rights and
   Obligations of the User" the licence is non-exclusive, "without the
   limitations of territory or period", and needs no further authorization
   and no fee.  The user may copy, publish and distribute the data "in
   whatever methods"; may apply it by any means that does not change its
   substantive meaning; and — this is the clause that matters most for
   OSM — may "usefully process the Data by means of either gathering the
   Data therefrom together with other data".  Combining it into somebody
   else's database is expressly contemplated.

   THIRD FINDING — AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT MOVES THE GATE.  There is NO
   share-alike clause, NO copyleft, NO restriction on sublicensing, and NO
   restriction on redistributing under a different licence.  The single
   standing obligation is attribution: the user "shall state references for
   the Use of Data, at all the time".  And where the producing organization
   cannot be identified individually, the Terms PRESCRIBE A BLANKET LINE:
       "The right to Use the Data is subject to the terms and conditions
        for DGA Open Government License"
   A source-level statement, not per-record attribution.  That is exactly
   the shape of attribution OSM gives — a contributors page naming the
   source — and per-record attribution was the reason I flagged CC-BY as a
   blocker in the first draft of this note.  The licence answers the
   objection in its own words.

   Exceptions listed: data the law forbids disclosing; logos, emblems and
   national-identity symbols; military insignia and uniform; and third-party
   IP DGA has no power to license.  A temple's name, a school's name and a
   street address touch none of the four.

   WHAT IS STILL NOT SETTLED, AND I AM NOT ASSERTING IT IS.  Licence text
   clearing the obstacles is not the same as OSM saying yes.  Any import at
   this scale goes to OSM's own review, which wants an explicit written
   statement from the publisher rather than a third party's reading of a
   PDF.  But the ASK has shrunk from "please change your licence" to
   "please confirm what your licence already says", and it goes to ONE
   addressee — DGA, whose Terms these are and whose contact address is
   printed in the document (contact@dga.or.th, DGA Contact Center).
   That letter is drafted: notes/dga-osm-licence-letter-2026-09-05.txt.

   What tier C brings: name + address on 5,451 of 5,468, and 999 phones.
   Zero hours — the registers do not hold them.

THE FINDING THAT STINGS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The tok sen records — the whole point of the search-disease work, the thing
we most want the world's maps to know — are 19 records, tier A, and
EIGHTEEN OF THEM HAVE NO PIN.

OSM cannot accept a node with no location.  So the flagship give-back is
blocked not by licence and not by schema but by the pin gap (F8, go/pins).
The one pinned tok-sen record can go up tomorrow.  The other eighteen are a
geocoder away, and the geocoder is a fork Nan has not answered.

WHAT I DID NOT DO
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
No edit, no upload, no account, no changeset, and nothing sent.  I DID read
the DGA licence text (the 5-page English PDF of 10 June 2021) and the OBEC
dataset page, and the summary above is what those documents say.  I have not
read a separate gdcatalog terms page — gdcatalog.go.th/pages/terms-of-use is
a 404 — so OBEC's 1,077 rest on the dataset page pointing back to data.go.th
rather than on a terms document I have seen.

I am not asserting a legal conclusion.  What I am asserting is narrower and
checkable: the licence text contains no share-alike clause, no sublicensing
restriction, an express permission to combine the data with other data, and
an attribution requirement its own text says may be met with a single blanket
sentence.  Whether that is sufficient for OSM is OSM's call, and the letter
is how we ask.
