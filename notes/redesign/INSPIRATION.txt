1. Flood Monitor — River Ping, Chiang Mai (AwareAI)
   https://flood.awareai.io/   ·   Thai: https://flood.awareai.io/th
   Sent by Michael via Nan, 2026-09-28:
   "it's so clear and easy to read, I think they're doing it better than you."
   For: motdang.net front page and every minisite.

   What it does

   The answer comes first, in one line.
      "Low flood risk. The Ping is well below its banks and unlikely to top
      them in the next 48 hours." A green dot, a bold verdict, one plain
      sentence. Everything after it is the evidence.

   Two numbers, big, each with a small caps label under it.
      1.33 m NOW AT NAWARAT BRIDGE · 1.38 m FORECAST PEAK · 48H
      Monospace digits, so they line up and read as measurements.

   A word before a percentage.
      "Unlikely" in colour, then "under 1% through +48 h" in small type.

   One scale for time: now · +12 · +24 · +48 h, drawn as a line with dots.

   A map beside the answer, with a five-step legend
      (below bank · <0.3 m · 0.3–0.7 m · 0.7–1.2 m · >1.2 m).

   Sections in order of how soon they matter:
      river now → 7-day chart → map → rain upstream, 5 days → flash flood,
      next 24 h → the dam → cameras → sources.

   Each section: a title, then one line saying its scope.
      "Upstream Outlook / Rain in the hills, 5 days ahead"
      "Flash Flood Risk / Possibility of sudden local flooding from intense
      rain, next 24 h"

   Cause, then effect, in a sentence a neighbour would say.
      "Rain this heavy can lift the river through the city within about a
      day." Then it points to the authority: "The official gauges show the
      actual level."

   Detail folds away: "Show the data behind this".

   Sources last, one line each, each with its link
      (Royal Irrigation Department, RID Reservoirs, Northern Hydrology
      Center, CMU flood poles).

   "Last updated: 17:34 ICT" top right. EN/TH switch top right, with a
   separate /th address.

   The method lives on its own page (/methodology), marked "Preliminary",
   and says what a gauge-only model cannot see.

   Dark navy ground, one accent blue, status colours only where status is.

   What Mot Dang takes

   Front page: open with the one-line answer for the reader's hour and
   city, then the two or three numbers that back it, then the rest.

   Minisites: a status line first; big numbers with small labels; sections
   ordered by how soon they matter; each section title followed by one
   scope line; "data behind this" folded; sources last, one line each.

   Nearest match on Mot Dang today: /nam.html and /sites/safety/ (the Ping
   gauge). Those two are the first to rebuild in this shape.

   Data they draw on, and where it comes from (checked 2026-09-28)

   The site states no licence or terms, and its script is obfuscated. The
   cross-check below read their pages; nothing of theirs is published.
      River levels   RID telemetry, telerid.rid.go.th (Mot Dang reads the
                     Upper North hydrology centre, hydro-1.net)
      Dams           RID reservoir API, app.rid.go.th/reservoir/api — public,
                     35 dams, north region includes Mae Ngat, Mae Kuang,
                     Kiew Lom. Mot Dang has no dam data yet.
      Flood poles    CMU, 219 poles across the city with the 2024 maximum
                     depth at each
      Rain           ECMWF forecast, RainViewer radar

   Cross-check against data/ping.json, 2026-09-28:
      Levels agree hour for hour (P.1 1.40 m at 14:00 in both).
      Two gauges carry different village names in the two RID systems:
         P.20  hydro-1: บ้านเชียงดาว   telerid: บ้านเกาะ
         P.21  hydro-1: บ้านเหมืองผ่า   telerid: บ้านริมใต้
      Telerid carries coordinates for 8 of our 10 gauges; ping.json has
      none. P.4A and P.82 are not in their list.
      Telerid reuses codes across basins: its "P.103" sits near Nakhon
      Sawan (16.09 N), not our P.103.
