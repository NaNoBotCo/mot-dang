2. THE POST — title, then body
--------------------------------------------------------------------
TITLE
88,000 places mapped, opening hours on 17% of them — where would you spend next?

BODY
I keep a city directory for Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai on my own — 88,228
places, Thai and English side by side, static HTML on R2 behind a Cloudflare
Worker, stdlib Python end to end. https://motdang.net

What I got wrong for most of a year: I built retrieval before I had fields
worth retrieving. The repo holds three engines — a structured/geo API over a
baked index at 120 ms, semantic search on Vectorize with bge-m3, and a
browser-side lexical index — and until this month the search box called the
weakest of the three. Wiring up the good one moved less than I expected,
because of this (measured 7 September, on the 25,914 canonical records):

  pin           97.7%
  address       42%
  English name  37%
  phone         20%
  hours         17%
  website       8.9%

Nothing ranks a fact that was never collected. The engine was not the ceiling.

Two findings that might carry to anyone working outside Latin script:

bge-m3 reads Thai, which matters more than it sounds. Thai is written
without spaces between words, so a lexical index over it is guessing at word
boundaries, and name search fails in ways an English test suite will never
show you.

OpenStreetMap is a weak first source here. Of the 24,059 records I had at
the time of that count, 63% came from OSM, 10% carried a website of any
kind, and 1% carried a Facebook page — while in this city a shop's Facebook
page is usually the only place it speaks for itself. OSM answers "what did a
mapper walk past," which is a different question from "who is open and
selling this."

Revenue is zero.

Where I would like the critique:

1. I am about to log queries for three weeks and then spend on fields rather
   than infrastructure. If you have run a directory: did the logs tell you
   what to collect, or did you end up collecting whatever was cheapest?

2. Hours and phone are the two fields readers want and the two hardest to
   get — owner-submitted, walked, or bought, each has a failure mode. What
   worked for you at a few thousand records?

3. The site shows its own gaps: a rank per listing counting what is missing,
   dead official links named and dated. My read is that this earns more
   trust than hiding them costs. I would like that argued against.
