1. F2 — RETRIEVER-FIRST, AND WHAT "FOR NOW" BUYS
==============================================================================

  Retriever-first means the resolved question goes to the edge (structured
  API + Vectorize) and searchcore becomes the fallback, not the front line.

  Kept anyway, deliberately, and this is what makes "might change later"
  cheap rather than a rewrite:

    · searchcore.js stays, and parity.py stays green. It is the offline path
      and the instant-name path, and it is the thing that keeps working when
      the edge does not.
    · The resolver's output — WHAT / WHERE / WHEN / WHICH / WORDS — is the
      stable interface. Which engine answers is a routing decision behind it.
      Changing the mix later is a config change, not a rebuild.
    · Every engine's answer is logged with its score (§3), so in three months
      the question "is the retriever still earning its place" has an answer
      from data rather than from taste.

2. F5 — INFRASTRUCTURE VS FIELDS, WITH THE NUMBERS
==============================================================================

  MEASURED TODAY, from data/canonical/{cm,cr}.json — 25,914 records:

      a pin              25,319   97.7%   ← the one thing we have
      an address         10,896   42.0%
      an English name     9,681   37.4%
      a phone             5,283   20.4%
      opening hours       4,402   17.0%
      trade tags          2,598   10.0%
      a website           2,313    8.9%
      a description       2,531 of 19,015 cm records (13.3%)

  Read that column downward. We know WHERE almost everything is and almost
  nothing ELSE about most of it. Four fifths of the directory cannot tell a
  reader when it opens or how to ring it.

  THE ARGUMENT FOR FIELDS
    No retriever can rank a fact that was never collected. Every phase in the
    main spec makes the site better at FINDING; none of them makes a car park
    with no hours into an answer to "is it open now". The wrong-tool note
    already measured the sharp end of this: of 1,796 car parks, 0 carry a
    phone and 2 carry hours, so 68% of them offer a reader nothing beyond the
    pin they can already see on the map.

  THE ARGUMENT AGAINST SPENDING IT YET — AND THIS IS THE REAL POINT
    You cannot target field-filling until you log queries. "The top 500
    most-queried records" is, today, unknowable: the box logs nothing. Money
    spent now buys fields chosen by guess.

    So F3 is not a parallel decision to F5. It is a PREREQUISITE for it.

      week 0      ship logging (2 hours of work, no new cost)
      weeks 1–3   let it run; the log says what people actually ask
      week 4      spend, on the fields the log named

    Three weeks of patience turns $100/month from a guess into a targeted
    buy. That is the whole recommendation.

  THE UNIT MATH, SO THE BUDGET MEANS SOMETHING
    Filling and CITING one record's hours + phone — find a first-hand source,
    verify it, enter it, record provenance — runs about 4 minutes when a
    source exists. Call it 15 records an hour.

      500 records  ≈ 33 hours
      1,000        ≈ 67 hours

    You know the Chiang Mai market for that work far better than I do, so I
    am giving you the work units rather than a wage. What I would say is that
    the first 500 should be chosen by the log, not by category.

  WHAT ELSE $100/MONTH COULD BUY, RANKED
    1. Targeted field-filling, chosen by the query log.        ← recommended
    2. Tavily, free tier or paid (~$30/mo at volume). It is what turns "the
       site could not answer this" into "and here is the place that exists".
       worker.js already reads env.TAVILY_KEY; there is nothing to build.
    3. A geocoder for the 2,116 unpinned records — LocationIQ (~$0–49/mo) or
       self-hosted Photon. Its own project, not this one.
    4. Infrastructure. Costs under $10/month all-in and is already bought.
       There is nothing here worth $100.

  AND THE CHEAPEST FIELDS OF ALL, WHICH ARE NOT A PURCHASE
    Your own directive of 9/6 stands: a place's own Facebook page IS
    first-hand, and 63% of records are OSM while only 1% carry an FB page.
    The blocker recorded there was MECHANISM — 0 of 5 logged-out fetches
    returned a fact — not policy. Solve the mechanism and a bot fills fields
    at zero marginal cost, which is §3's F4 tier line exactly.
    That is the highest-leverage unsolved problem on this list.

3. F3 — WHAT GETS LOGGED, AND WHAT NEVER DOES
==============================================================================

  ONE row per resolved query, POSTed to the existing Worker.

  WRITTEN:
    ts                  when
    source              box │ map │ chip │ tag │ voice
    q_raw               what they typed, verbatim, capped at 300 chars
    resolved            the structure: {what, where, when, which, words}
    unknown             any word the resolver could not place  ← Cause 2
    engines             which of A/B/C answered, and each one's top score
    n                   how many results came back
    top                 the id ranked first
    outcome             opened:<id> │ refined │ mapped │ abandoned

  NOT IN THE SCHEMA:
    no IP · no seat id · no cookie · no fingerprint · no account link.
    search_log already carries no seat id, on purpose — the existing schema
    comment says it plainly: "this is about the sites' coverage, not about
    people." The new column does not change that.

  RETENTION: rows roll off at 180 days; the aggregates that feed the bots
  survive. D1's free tier covers this at a hundred times current traffic.

  DISCLOSED: one line on terms.html. The licence page already makes promises
  about this data and it should not have to be amended later.

  WHY `unknown` IS THE MOST VALUABLE COLUMN
    "moto" was not in the thesaurus, so one shop 4.6 km away was reported
    with total confidence. A log of the words we did not understand is a
    ranked to-do list for the vocabulary, and it is generated by readers
    rather than guessed by us.


==============================================================================
4. F4 — THE BOTS ACT. HERE IS THE LINE THEY DO NOT CROSS.
==============================================================================

  ONE RULE, and everything else follows from it:

      Does the action ASSERT A NEW FACT ABOUT THE WORLD,
      or does it only improve the site's ability to find facts it
      already holds?

      The second, a bot does. The first, Nan signs.

  TIER 1 — ACT. Logged, reversible, no ask.
    · add a synonym, a thesaurus group, or a segdict word
      (this is the `unknown` column becoming a fix, unattended)
    · build a shelf page for records that exist with nowhere to sit
      (34 of 175 shelves are at zero; 73 records fill 4 of them off their
      own names)
    · re-run a crawl for a category that returned nothing
    · re-embed changed records into Vectorize
    · merge exact-id duplicates; fix a broken internal link or a bad slug
    · compute a bearing or a landmark relation from pins ALREADY HELD
    · open, rank and close work orders

  TIER 2 — PROPOSE. Nan signs.
    · publish a NEW place record — that asserts a place exists
    · write or change any field that states a fact about a business:
      hours, phone, "treats X", price, whether it is open
    · anything touching licence or provenance — the OSM give-back, the 3,303
      forbidden rows, the DGA/OGL boundary
    · delete or unpublish anything
    · change what a register or lens CLAIMS about a place

  THE GUARDRAILS
    · A work order needs a THRESHOLD, never a single query: seen ≥ 3, or a
      cluster of phrasings that resolve to one want. One person's typo is
      not a mandate.
    · Every Tier-1 act writes a provenance row naming the bot, the trigger
      query, and the timestamp. `git revert`-able, one command.
    · A Tier-1 action firing more than 20 times in a day PAUSES and asks.
      A bot that has found a hundred things to fix has found a bug.
    · A weekly digest: what acted, what is waiting on you, what it cost.
    · Absence stays absence. A bot may never fill a field with a guess to
      make a page look complete — `openNow` is null and
      never false applies to bots exactly as it applies to the API.


==============================================================================
5. F6 — STRUCK, AND WHAT IT UNLOCKS
==============================================================================

  Nan: "I never said the website needed to be purely static."

  She did not. I wrote that constraint into F6 myself and attributed the
  caution to the project. Struck. What it opens up, in order of value:

    · SERVER-RENDER THE FIRST ANSWER. The answer arrives in the HTML.
      No 1.5 MB, no JavaScript wait, no blank screen on Thai mobile data.
      This is a better fix than Phase 4's "smaller index" and it is simpler.
    · PER-QUESTION INDEXES AT THE EDGE. The Worker already parses one baked
      file per isolate and answers in 120 ms. A reader never downloads a
      catalogue again.
    · REAL QUERY ENDPOINTS instead of a downloaded blob — which is what the
      /api/v1 Worker has been doing for a month already, for everyone except
      our own readers.
    · SESSION THINGS BECOME POSSIBLE: near me, my plan, recently viewed,
      the map remembering where you were.

  PHASE 4 IS THEREFORE REWRITTEN: it is no longer "make the index smaller".
  It is "render the answer at the edge and stop shipping an index at all",
  with the small local index kept deliberately for offline and instant
  name-matching. Same effort, much better outcome.

  ONE THING WORTH KEEPING ON PURPOSE, not as a constraint but as a feature:
  the site works on a phone with no signal once it has been loaded. That is
  worth something in a province with real dead zones, and it should survive
  as a deliberate choice about which pages cache — not as a rule about how
  the whole site is built.
