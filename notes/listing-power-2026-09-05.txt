MOT DANG — WHO MAY CHANGE A LISTING, AND HOW WE ASK
A design note, 2026-09-05. Written after reading worker/worker.js, not before.


WHAT ALREADY EXISTS
================================================================================
Half of what you described is built, and the model is the house one: capability
tokens, not accounts. Nobody signs up, nobody has a password.

  POST /claim          anyone, once per place, rate-limited by IP. Publishes
                       instantly and hands back a private edit link.
  GET/POST /edit/:tok  the holder's private link: phone, LINE, Facebook,
                       Instagram, WhatsApp, email, website, hours, note, menu.
  POST /toilet         a passer-by's answer to ONE question at one place. No
                       token, no claim, nothing locked. A deliberately
                       separate door.
  POST /suggest        a correction or a missing place, closed vocabulary, into
                       a queue that is NOT public because it carries the
                       contributor's own contact details.
  /webhook/line        the whole flow by chatting with the LINE account, through
                       the same applyClaim() the web routes use.
  GET /claims          public, folded into the static build by sync_claims.py.

And the authorisation instinct you just described is already written into the
code, in a comment:

    "Facets alone can never open a claim. Claiming locks the record against
     everyone else, so letting a passer-by do it by ticking 'has an ATM' would
     let a stranger lock a shop out of its own listing."

So the question is not whether people may edit. It is what happens in the three
cases the current code has no answer for.


THE THREE HOLES
================================================================================
1. A CONTESTED CLAIM HAS NOWHERE TO GO.  A second claimant gets
   409 "already claimed — if you think this is wrong, tell us on GitHub".
   A shop owner in Chiang Mai is not going to open a GitHub issue. First claim
   wins, forever, including when the first claimant is not the owner.

2. THERE IS NO WAY TO SAY A PLACE IS GONE.  No takedown at all — which is the
   right default and should stay — but also no authorised removal: a shop that
   closed, a record that is wrong, a person who wants off the site.

3. AN EDIT OVERWRITES.  applyClaim() writes the claim row straight over the old
   one in KV. There is no history, so there is nothing to roll back to. That is
   the one thing worth fixing BEFORE widening who may write, not after.


FOUR KINDS OF PERSON
================================================================================
The asymmetry you asked for, stated as a table. "How we know" is the whole
design: none of it is a profile on a server, all of it is either a token the
device holds or a fact about the record.

  PASSER-BY      we know nothing; rate-limited by IP
                 may: report one observable fact (a toilet, a facet tick, "this
                 looks closed"), suggest a correction, add a missing place,
                 share. Every one of these is a REPORT — dated, attributed to a
                 report, never a silent change to the page.

  CONTRIBUTOR    holds a token minted on their first accepted contribution
                 may: all of the above, and their reports land pre-trusted, and
                 they can see their own history. Optional — see fork F4.

  HOLDER         holds the edit link for that one place
                 may: edit contact, hours, note and menu on THAT record; mark it
                 temporarily or permanently closed; hand the link to a successor.
                 Nothing on any other record.

  STEWARD        an admin token (you)
                 may: resolve a contest, transfer a claim, retire a record,
                 restore anything to any earlier version.

The rule underneath, in one line: A PERSON MAY ALWAYS ADD WHAT THEY SAW, AND MAY
ONLY CHANGE WHAT IS THEIRS. Nobody but a steward can subtract from a listing
that someone else holds.


CONTEST, NOT TAKEOVER
================================================================================
Replace the GitHub dead end with a door that changes nothing until it is
verified:

  1. Second claimant gives a reachable channel (LINE or the phone on the
     listing) and one line of reason.
  2. That writes a contest: row and pings you. THE PAGE DOES NOT CHANGE.
  3. A claim is "verified" once it has been confirmed through a channel we can
     reach — a LINE reply from the account, or a code to the phone already on
     the record. Claims made today are all unverified.
  4. A VERIFIED claim supersedes an UNVERIFIED one automatically after a waiting
     period (F2). A verified claim can only be displaced by a steward.

That gives a real owner a path without letting anyone seize a record, and it
makes squatting expensive rather than permanent.

Removal has the same shape: a passer-by reports "closed", which renders as a
dated reader report and nothing more; a holder may mark their own place closed
and that is authoritative; only a steward retires the record itself, and even
then it is retired, not deleted — the antRank ledger and inbound links need it.


CALLS TO ACTION — ABUNDANT, NOT CLUTTERED
================================================================================
The constraint you set is the whole spec: abundant, presented by type of user,
hints that do not distort visual understanding. Four rules get there.

  ONE PRIMARY ACT PER PAGE, chosen by what the record LACKS, not by what we
  would like people to do. No phone on the record → "Add a phone". Claimed with
  a LINE id → "Message on LINE". Complete → "Share". The page never shows two
  competing asks.

  EVERYTHING ELSE BEHIND ONE QUIET DOOR. A single "Help fix this" that opens in
  place — and the space it will occupy is reserved before it opens, so nothing
  on the page moves. That is the literal reading of "does not distort visual
  understanding": no reflow, ever.

  HINTS, NOT TOOLTIPS. A tooltip needs a pointer to hover, and most readers here
  are on a phone with one hand on a scooter helmet. Use a persistent one-line
  hint under the primary act, in the reader's language, and keep title= and
  aria-describedby for the pointer users who will never see the hint.

  SHARE IS EVERYWHERE AND WEIGHTLESS. One affordance, on every place, every
  shelf, every answer: copies a short link with the place name and area already
  written, in the language the reader is in. Sharing is the only act that costs
  the reader nothing and gives the site everything, so it is the one CTA that
  never has to earn its place.

Type-awareness with no accounts: the page reads only what the DEVICE holds — an
edit token, a prior contribution, a language — so the CTAs shift locally and
nothing about a reader is ever stored on a server. That keeps the no-tracking
rule intact while still giving four different people four different pages.


THE ASSISTANT IS A DOOR TOO
================================================================================
คุยกับมด already answers about places. It could also ACT on them — "tell them I
am coming", "this one has closed", "add their LINE" — and it is the door most
likely to be used, because it is the one where the reader is already talking.
That is fork F5, and it is the largest of them.


WHAT I WOULD BUILD FIRST
================================================================================
  1. Versioned claims. Every write appends; nothing overwrites. Cheap now,
     impossible to retrofit once there is volume.
  2. The contest door, replacing the GitHub message.
  3. Holder-marked closed, and reader "looks closed" reports as dated reports.
  4. The CTA layer: one primary act, one quiet door, share everywhere.
  5. Verification, and only then the automatic supersede.


FORKS — YOURS, NOT MINE
================================================================================
  F1  Verification channel: LINE reply only, or LINE plus a code to the phone
      already on the listing? SMS needs a provider and a per-message cost.
  F2  How long a verified claim waits before it supersedes an unverified one:
      7, 14 or 30 days.
  F3  Do independent "looks closed" reports ever change the page on their own —
      say three in thirty days renders "readers report this closed" — or never
      without you?
  F4  Contributor tokens at all, or stay fully anonymous? Tokens make trust
      cumulative and make abuse traceable; they are also the first thing on this
      site that resembles an account.
  F5  Does the assistant get act-on-listing doors, or stay read-only?
