## CORRECTED THE SAME DAY, BY NAN

> *"with the number of kathoeys in CM, I thought it would be easier to find a
> large woman's going-out shoe."*

She is right, and the first pass was wrong — not in its numbers but in **whose
question it answered.** Every search behind it was framed for a man: เบอร์ 45 46
47 48, army stores, Decathlon, an EU 46 boot. A large **woman's** going-out shoe
is a different trade, with different numbers (**41–45**, not 45–48), a different
word (**ส้นสูงไซส์ใหญ่**, big-size *high heel* — not รองเท้าไซส์ใหญ่ on its own),
and a different customer. Ask the men's question, get the men's answer, and
publish it as the city's.

Her inference is also a demographic argument, and it holds: Chiang Mai has a
large and visible กะเทย / สาวสอง population and a working cabaret trade, so the
demand for a 42 in a heel is steady. Steady demand is supplied by somebody. The
first pass concluded *nobody stocks your size* from eight OpenStreetMap records
— and OSM was never the source that would know.

### What the corrected question returns

**Five women's shoe shops standing side by side in one aisle** — ล้านอ้อย,
ล้านอ้อย ๑, นุ้ยชูส์, ร้านแม่อ้อย, เพชรน้ำหนึ่ง — in โซนเครื่องแต่งกาย,
**ตลาดธานินทร์**, every one tagged รองเท้าส้นสูง by the directory, **all five
within thirty metres of each other**, three with phones, two updated
2025-11-27, all with their own published GPS. รสริน on ถ.สันติธรรม makes six.
Recorded as `cm-curated-heels-*`.

**Not one of the six was in the crawl.** A stall inside a covered market is not
a `shop=shoes` node.

**None of the six publishes a size ceiling** — checked, in each cached page. So
they are not an answer; they are a *place to ask*, and six counters in one walk
is still the best available shape of one. One sentence at each:
**ไซส์ใหญ่สุดเบอร์อะไร**.

### And the trade names its customer

A Thai retailer selling women's shoes from **35 to 49**, with a ส้นสูงไซส์ใหญ่
line at 41–45 and heels to eight inches, writes on its own product pages:
*สำหรับผู้ที่เท้าอวบอูมหรือสาวสอง* — for people with full feet, or สาวสอง — and
publishes a foot chart for 40–46 with the advice to size up one. So the shoe is
made and sold in this country in plain retail Thai. It does not settle Chiang
Mai: it is online, and a shoe is the one thing not to buy without standing up
in it.

### The correction this forces on the page

"Eight named shoe shops in the whole of Chiang Mai" is **true of OpenStreetMap
and false about the city.** CMHY's รองเท้า category alone holds about ninety.
A count taken from a source that cannot see the thing you asked about is not a
count, and the page now says so in its own first note.

**`gap: true` is gone.** The gap rule exists so a shareable poster never asserts
an absence; this page no longer asserts one. It has a list, a card, and an aisle.

### The lead that is worth more than all the rest

**Ask the people who already know — and this directory already holds all four.**
มูลนิธิเอ็มพลัส MPlus (`cm-curated-mplus-chiangmai`, in the trans-health register
at grade `stated`), ศูนย์สุขภาพแคร์แมท CAREMAT, มูลนิธิยังไพรด์ Young Pride Club,
and Chiang Mai Cabaret Theatre (`cm-osm-way-762611799`) — whose performers need
42–45 in a heel every working night. One question, four contacts already on the
site, and the women's half of this question stops being a method.

It must be asked as a question and never published as an assumption. This file
does not claim that the Tanin row carries a 43, and does not claim that it is
where สาวสอง in Chiang Mai buy. Neither is known, and writing either in this
directory's voice — a claim about a community's own shopping, on no evidence —
is exactly the error the register exists to refuse. **The answer belongs on the
page in their words.**

## Why this is not the same question as the clothes one

A shirt can be **made**. This directory learned 67 tailors yesterday and any of
them will cut to a body no shelf carries, which is why the clothes question ends
in a choice between buying and commissioning.

**A shoe cannot** — not at a price anyone would pay for one pair. So the clothes
page is a list of doors and this page is mostly a *method*, and folding the two
would let the fuller half hide how thin this one is. Same rule as pest/snake:
two problems that arrive in one sentence and separate the moment you answer them.

## The measurement

`shop=shoes`: **9 in Chiang Mai, 9 in Chiang Rai** in the census of 2026-08-07,
and **zero** in the catalogue, because the selector had never been asked for. The
shelf now holds **8 in Chiang Mai and 4 in Chiang Rai** — named only, which is the
whole of it.
The new crawl of 2026-08-31 returns **8 named shoe shops in the whole of Chiang
Mai**, and here they are, all of them:

> Bata · Bata · Bata · Skechers · Sasi Shoes · findig · ลูกหมี · แตะพี่หมอ

Three of the eight are the same Thai chain and one sells sandals. **That is the
shoe retail of a city of a million people as the open map holds it**, and it is
not a shelf that answers this question. CMHY.city's own รองเท้า category holds
about 50 more and they are the brand counters in the malls — Super Sport, Nike
Factory Store, Adidas outlet, Converse, New Balance, Bata, Crocs, Puma, Skechers,
FILA. **Not one shop in either source advertises a large size.**

That is not this order failing to find them. It is the answer.

## The answer is a number, not an address

**Measure your foot in centimetres and say that number.**

Stand on a sheet of paper, mark the heel and the longest toe, measure between the
marks. The centimetre means the same thing in every shop in Thailand, in every
language, on every shelf. **เบอร์ does not** — and the proof is that two published
Thai charts read on the same day disagree:

| foot | Mizuno Thailand | ไทยรัฐ (25 Jul 2023) |
|---|---|---|
| 28.0 cm | 44 | 44 |
| 29.0 cm | 45 | 45 |
| 29.5 cm | 45.5 | **46** |
| 30.0 cm | **46** | 46.5 |
| 30.5 cm | 46.5 | **47** |
| 31.0 cm | **47** | — |
| 32.0 cm | 48.5 | — |

They agree exactly at 44 and 45 and part company above them. **Half a centimetre
is a full size.** Past 45, say the centimetre.

A third table, the first one an ordinary search returns, gives **EU 44 = 24.6 cm**
— about a 39, three and a half sizes wrong. It is not used here and it is
recorded because it is the shape of the hazard.

*A conversion chart is the maker's, never the country's.*

## The routes that do exist, in the order a reader should try them

1. **A sports or outdoor shop, not a shoe shop.** The chains buy from
   international size runs and stock past where a fashion shelf stops. The crawl
   found Decathlon ×3, Adidas, Sports Maximise, สปอร์ตแมกซ์, แสนทองสปอร์ต, APX
   Sport & Lifestyle, The Highlandner Runner Stop, and an **Army Stores** on the
   outdoor tag. This is why the answer to "where do I find a 46" in this city is
   more often a sports shop than a shoe shop — and why `shopping/sports-shop`,
   which had exactly one record and no selector behind it, is part of this order.
2. **Ask them to order it in.** *สั่งได้ไหม กี่วัน* — can you order it, how many
   days. A brand counter that has no 46 on the wall can very often get one.
3. **Online.** Where the Thai big-size shoe trade actually lives; แตะไซส์ใหญ่
   sandals run to เบอร์ 52 there and nowhere on a Chiang Mai street.
4. **Have them made.** Real and expensive. WO-49 found a bootmaker filed on the
   tailor shelf by mistake — the trade exists here. It is not comparable in price
   to any of the above and the page says so rather than pretending it is.

## Decision

- **`gap: true`.** The shelf `shopping/shoes` is created and filled with what the
  city has, and the question is published as a gap: eight named shoe shops in a
  province, none advertising a size, and the honest answer is a method plus four
  routes. **No share card** — a poster reading "nobody stocks your size" travels
  further than the sentence under it.
- shelf: `shoes` (รองเท้า) and `sports-shop` (ชุดกีฬา-อุปกรณ์กีฬา), added under `shopping`
- facet: none. A size ceiling read off a rail would earn one; nothing has been.
- NOT recorded: any claim that a named shop carries a 46. Not one publishes it,
  and putting it there on our authority is how a reader drives across town for
  nothing.
- NOT recorded as a fact, kept in the register: that a Thai shelf normally stops
  at about 44 for men and 40–41 for women. Widely true, nobody here has counted.
