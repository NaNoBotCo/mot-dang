────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHY, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
`sources[]` says where a RECORD came from.  Nothing said where a FIELD came
from, and two jobs need exactly that: OpenStreetMap asks its question one tag
at a time (go/osm-gate), and `offers[]` is a claim that needs a receipt
(Phase 3 of the cure plan).  Both want the same sentence — this fact, from
that source, in this way — so they now get one grammar instead of two.

This is not a new idea in the repo; it is an existing one finished.  The pin
has carried `attrs.pinSource` / `pinVia` / `pinFrom` / `pinUncertaintyM` for
a while, and `facet_bits()` already returns `{key: provenance}` rather than a
flat true.  Two ad-hoc receipts for two fields somebody got burned on.  This
generalises them rather than adding a third dialect.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE SHAPE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    record["provenance"] = {
      "phone": {"source": "opendata:data.go.th",  "how": "stated",   "at": "2026-08-20"},
      "hours": {"source": "web:venue-site",       "how": "stated",   "at": "2026-08-19"},
      "lat":   {"source": "derived:curated-pins", "how": "inferred", "at": "2026-09-05"},
      "sub":   {"source": "curated:shelves.json", "how": "derived",  "at": "2026-09-05"},
    }

`source` is a stable KEY (`type:via`), never an index into `sources[]` — the
`apply_*` passes append to that list after the merge, so an index written on
Tuesday points somewhere else on Wednesday.

THREE GRADES, and the whole safety mechanism is that there are three:
  stated    the source printed this field, in these words
  derived   we computed it from what the source stated (a shelf off a name)
  inferred  we filled it within stated bounds and could be wrong (a geocode)
ONLY `stated` is ever giveable.  `derived` is our reading and `inferred` is
our guess, and neither is a fact somebody surveyed.

ABSENCE IS ABSENCE.  A field with no entry has UNKNOWN provenance — not
"ours", not "OSM".  Every consumer fails closed: a wrong refusal costs one
record left at home, a wrong permission costs an upload we cannot defend.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TWO GATES, HELD APART ON PURPOSE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
`giveable(record, field)` answers "may this go to OSM TODAY".
`needs_licence_clearance(record, field)` answers "is this held up only by a
letter nobody has answered yet".

They were one function for about ten minutes, and that version would have
green-lit all 6,150 government-register fields before DGA replied — which is
precisely the promise the letter makes (go/dga-letter).  So the promise now
lives in code as a constant:

    LICENCE_CLEARED = (OURS, VENUE)      # GOV goes in the day the reply lands

and `tests/test_provenance.py` fails if somebody adds GOV to it.  A promise a
test can enforce is worth more than a promise in a note.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT THE BACKFILL CAN HONESTLY DO — AND WHAT IT REFUSES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
94% of the corpus carries EXACTLY ONE SOURCE — 22,652 of 24,059 records.  For
those, provenance is not a guess: there was nowhere else a field could have
come from.  One pass, 143,500 entries:

    stated 89,569  ·  derived 42,729  ·  inferred 11,202

The other 1,407 records carry two or more sources.  The backfill fills only
what an existing MARKER settles (the pin) and leaves every other field
UNKNOWN.  Deliberately.  A rule like "the phone probably came from the
register" would be right most of the time, and provenance that is right most
of the time is worse than none — it READS as a receipt, it gets uploaded on
the strength of looking like one, and the day it is wrong nothing can tell you
which day that was.

    fields giveable to OSM today       359
    fields waiting on a licence reply  16,054
    fields still unaccounted           the 1,407 records' 9,284

And those 1,407 are the same 1,407 that hold tier B of the give-back — the
373 records where we have enriched an OSM object with our own work.  So the
backlog is not diffuse: it is one hand-checkable list, and it is the list that
was worth doing this for.
