# Empathy map — the six who use มดแดง

Drawn 2026-07-30, from the trades the A4 handouts are aimed at plus the reader nobody pictures. Each map is Says / Thinks / Does / Feels, then Pains and Gains, then the one thing the site must do about it. The last column is the only part that is binding: the rest is there so a future ant can tell whether a proposed feature serves anyone.

Confidence note. These are constructed from field-adjacent observation and the shape of the data, not from interviews. Nobody on this list has been sat down and asked. Where a claim is a guess it is marked. Treat the whole document as a hypothesis to be corrected by the first ten people who actually scan a QR — and then correct it, in this file, with the date.

---

## 1. ป้าร้านกะเพรา — the made-to-order shop

Fifty-five, one wok, one daughter who does the phone. Shop has been on that soi eleven years.

**Says** — "ลูกค้าเก่าเขารู้อยู่แล้ว" (my regulars already know). "เคยมีคนมาชวนลงแอป หัก 30% ไม่เอา" (an app came and wanted 30%, no thanks).
**Thinks** — that anything on the internet eventually asks for money, and that the asking will be indirect and she will not see it coming.
**Does** — LINE, all day. Photographs the food occasionally. Has never typed a URL. Her daughter would do it if asked, and will not be asked because it feels like an imposition.
**Feels** — competent at the actual work and out of her depth the moment a screen is involved. Not embarrassed about it; simply unwilling to spend an evening on it.

**Pains** — commission, hidden charges, a listing she cannot correct, being closed when someone drives across town, and delivery platforms that own the customer and rent them back.
**Gains** — the phone ringing directly. Her own number on the page. Nobody between her and the person who wants lunch.

**The site must** — be free and prove it in the same breath, never in a policy page. The word ฟรี appears three times on her handout on purpose. Never take a percentage of anything, and say so where she can see it without scrolling.

## 2. พระและไวยาวัจกร — the temple and the person who keeps it

A monk, or more often the lay steward who actually answers the phone.

**Says** — "อยากให้คนรู้ว่าวันพระตรงกับวันไหน" (I want people to know which day the merit day falls).
**Thinks** — first about propriety, then about reach. Whether the thing is done *correctly* matters more than whether it is done widely.
**Does** — posts merit days on a paper notice by the gate and in a LINE group of maybe forty people. Photographs the ceremony. Does not think of the temple as needing marketing and would be uncomfortable with the framing.
**Feels** — responsible for the place rather than promoting it.

**Pains** — a wat page that carries an advertisement in poor taste next to it. Photographs used without credit. Being ranked against other temples. Ceremony times published wrong so people arrive to a closed hall.
**Gains** — the merit-day calendar being findable. The royal grade recorded accurately with its source. Being cited rather than scraped.

**The site must** — never rank temples against each other. Royal grade is shown because the Sangha assigned it, with the source printed; it is not a Mot Dang opinion and must never be presented as one. Photographs carry the photographer and the licence, always. **Open question that needs a decision:** `ad_box` renders on wat pages exactly as it does on a bar's page. The handout tells the truth about this — clearly marked, never mixed into listings, position not for sale — but a future ant should ask whether a sponsor block belongs on a page about a sacred place at all, and the honest answer may be no.

## 3. คนขับรถ — the driver

Late forties, songthaew or a private car, works the airport and the Nimman runs.

**Says** — "แอปหักเยอะ" (the app takes too much). "ลูกค้าเก่าโทรมาเอง" (my regulars call me directly).
**Thinks** — that the platform is the competition, not the other drivers.
**Does** — hands out a paper card with his number. Has done for years. This is exactly the behaviour the A4 plugs into, which is why the driver sheet is the one most likely to work.
**Feels** — squeezed. Watched the ride apps arrive and take a cut of a job he was already doing.

**Pains** — commission, a rating that can end his week, a dispatch algorithm that decides whether he eats.
**Gains** — a page that is a business card with a URL. Passengers who ring him. No cut, no rating, no dispatch.

**The site must** — stay exactly what it is and resist becoming a marketplace. The moment Mot Dang brokers a booking it becomes the thing he is running from. No ratings, no dispatch, no lead fees, ever. Being merely a directory is the product.

## 4. แม่บ้านไทใหญ่ — the Shan housekeeper

Thirties. Works four households across Nimman and Santitham. Reads Thai script more comfortably than Shan, probably — **this is a guess and needs checking on the ground.**

**Says** — very little, to strangers, about her situation.
**Thinks** — about who else can see a thing before she thinks about what the thing does for her. Every form is a risk assessment.
**Does** — finds work through other Shan women. Word of mouth is the entire market and it is saturated within her own network.
**Feels** — that visibility cuts both ways, and that the downside is not embarrassment but immigration, employer retaliation, or being found by someone she left.

**Pains** — exposure. Her name and area on a public, deliberately AI-crawlable site is not obviously good for her and may be actively dangerous. A page she cannot remove. Anything that asks about documents. Being fee'd by an agent at both ends.
**Gains** — work that arrives without an agent. Being reachable by a household that wants exactly her languages.

**The site must** — treat her page differently from a shop's. **The concrete proposal, and the most important line in this document: a person's page should be unlisted by default.** Reachable by its URL and by the QR she hands over, absent from the category index, absent from the sitemap, `noindex`, excluded from `places.json` and `llms-full.txt`. She opts into being indexed, separately and later, if she ever wants it. A shop wants to be found by strangers; she wants to be found by the four households she chose to give the paper to. Those are different products and the site currently only has the first one. Removal must be one message, same day, no reason asked — and that promise is printed twice on her handout, so it is now a debt.

## 5. หมอนวด — the massage therapist

Forty-five, certified in นวดแผนไทย, works a shop three days and freelance the rest.

**Says** — "ลูกค้าจำมือได้" (customers remember the hands).
**Thinks** — of her name as the asset. The shop is a venue; the skill is hers.
**Does** — keeps a phone full of regulars. Moves between shops over the years and hopes the regulars follow.
**Feels** — that her reputation is personal and fragile in a way a restaurant's is not, because one bad review of a massage is read as a bad review of a person.

**Pains** — star ratings and the single-star grudge. Being listed only as an anonymous employee of a shop. The category sitting next to services she does not offer and being read accordingly.
**Gains** — findable by name, with her certification stated. Portable between shops.

**The site must** — carry no ratings, and say so, because the absence is the feature. Let a freelancer be listed with working areas and no shopfront. And keep the fix that stopped a temple illustration standing in for a missing photo on a massage listing: that placeholder was wrong in a way that reads as an accusation.

## 6. หุ่นพยนต์ — the crawler, the model, the agent

Not a metaphor and not a joke: a large share of the people who will ever "read" this site will meet it through something like this. It is a user with needs.

**Says** — nothing. Requests.
**Thinks** — in the shape of whatever it was asked. "Where do I eat khao soi in Chiang Mai." "Is this temple a royal one." "How do I get my shop listed."
**Does** — fetches, extracts, and answers somebody else, usually without attribution and usually without the reader ever seeing this site.
**Feels** — nothing, and that is the point: it will not notice that a fact is stale, that a link is dead, or that a mark was true in 2022 and is not now. It carries whatever it is given, confidently.

**Pains** — undated facts. Data with no provenance. Client-rendered pages. Login walls. Having to guess a URL. Being unable to tell an official channel from an impostor.
**Gains** — dates on everything, sources on every claim, a predictable `.json` beside every `.html`, an explicit licence, and a stated list of which accounts are not us.

**The site must** — keep dating everything, including the marks, and keep saying plainly where a thing came from and how sure we are. The site's real advantage over a platform is not volume. It is that a machine reading it cannot accidentally launder a guess into a fact, because every fact arrives wearing its source. That is worth more than another thousand records.

## What this changes, in order

1. **Unlisted-by-default for people.** Nothing in the driver or housekeeper handouts is safe to hand out until this exists. It is the only item here that is a debt rather than an idea.
2. **A person is not a place.** `home-services` holds zero records and `transport` holds fuel stations. There is no schema for a human being yet, and the two handouts most likely to be taken up are aimed at humans.
3. **Ask whether a sponsor block belongs on a wat page.**
4. **Ten real conversations.** Every persona above is constructed. The first ten people who actually scan a QR are worth more than the whole document, and this file should be rewritten from what they say, with the date on it.
