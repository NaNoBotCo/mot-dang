FOUR FAULTS. THE FOURTH ONE WAS FOUND WHILE FIXING THE OTHER THREE.
==============================================================================

1. THE WORD "GATE" OPENED THE ELEPHANT DOOR.
   The search page's shelf table is MINED from each shelf's English teaser.
   The elephant teaser reads "...the mahouts, the white elephant, the gate and
   the chedis that carry its name, the elephant clinic, poo paper...". Split on
   whitespace, that taught the table: gate, white, carry, states, about,
   chedis, riding → ELEPHANTS. Every gate in this city is a landmark people
   navigate by. The same sentence had already done this once, with `clinic`,
   in August; that was patched one word at a time.

2. A FAILED SEARCH STILL WORE A DOOR.
   The engine had already worked out that nothing matched all four words. It
   rendered the topic panel anyway, above its own confession, and printed
   "Search results 3,514" — the size of the loosened pile, not a count of
   answers.

3. THE CATALOGUE HELD NO PARKING AT ALL.
   Zero among 22,351 places. OpenStreetMap has held the answer since before
   this site existed and no crawl group had ever asked for the tag.

4. THE WORD HE TYPED FIRST NEVER REACHED THE INDEX.
   searchcore reads "parking" as a CONSTRAINT and eats it out of the query.
   So the engine searched for *motorcycle + near + taphae + gate* — his first
   word, the whole point of the question, was deleted before anything was
   matched. Worse, the same rule meant that typing ANY constraint on its own —
   parking, wifi, open now, wheelchair, ที่จอดรถ — returned ZERO rows sitewide.
   Not a parking bug: a general one, live for as long as intent parsing has
   been in.

WHAT WAS BUILT
==============================================================================

FIX 1 — a mined shelf can no longer open a door.
  · build.py: the panel trigger now reads variants and query phrases only.
    `shelves` keeps its other job (lifting rows, splitting namesakes); it was
    one field doing two things and only the trigger was leaky.
  · The old rule asked curators to use a shelf trigger "only when every mined
    word names the topic". That is not a check a person can make — the words
    are mined, they change when a teaser is reworded, and they are invisible
    in the file. It failed twice on one sentence, so the kind is retired.
  · search-core/mine.py: an English teaser is read as a LIST, not as prose.
    Items carrying a clause word, a determiner, or more than three words are
    not mined. Measured over the 175 shelves: 17 junk terms dropped (`gate`
    and `clinic` among them), 1 real one lost (`curry`, out of a sentence).
  · 49 real topic words the retired trigger used to catch were restored by
    hand as panel variants — ปางช้าง, ควาญ, ค่ายมวย, สนามมวย, ตอกเส้น, ย่ำขาง,
    barbers, salons, condos. The junk was not.
  · 15 residue words stopped by name in search_panels.json.

FIX 2 — when the search fails, it says so first.
  · No panel renders in partial mode.
  · The "nothing matches all of…" and "understood X" lines move ABOVE
    everything, at body size, directly under the box.
  · The heading reads "Search results 3,931 · partial matches".
  · A stuck reader gets one line offering the ants (who read whole sentences)
    and a crawl request, with the query carried into both.

FIX 3 — the parking shelf. 1,796 records where there were none.
  · New crawl group, province-wide in both provinces: amenity=parking,
    motorcycle_parking, bicycle_parking, parking_entrance.
       Chiang Mai 1,361     Chiang Rai 435
  · New shelf ที่จอดรถ · Parking under Getting Around, with children for
    motorcycle, bicycle and entrances. A motorbike bay carries both words, so
    "motorcycle parking" is a shelf query, not a name search.
  · UNNAMED LOTS PUBLISH — your call, and it is 1,711 of the 1,796. They are
    unnamed because there is no signboard, not because nobody got round to it.
    Each is named from what it does carry (the structure, the vehicle, the
    operator) and then given the one fact that tells it apart, from the
    bearings layer:

        ที่จอดรถ · ใกล้ประตูท่าแพ 80 ม.       Parking · 80 m from Tha Phae Gate
        อาคารจอดรถ · ใกล้ประตูท่าแพ 220 ม.    Multi-storey · 220 m from Tha Phae
        ลานจอดรถ · ย่านแม่ริม                 Car park · Mae Rim

    1,039 distinct names for 1,796 records; the repeats are rural lots sharing
    a district name, far from any landmark.
  · A NAME WE WROTE SAYS SO. Every synthesised name is stamped `derived` in
    per-field provenance, so the OSM give-back can never offer them a name for
    a lot their mappers left bare on purpose. A lot a mapper DID name keeps it,
    stamped `stated`.
  · Facts kept where OSM states them: fee, access, capacity, structure,
    surface, covered, supervised, lit, maxstay, charge, park-and-ride,
    whether a car park takes motorbikes. 792 of 1,796 carry at least one.
    Absent stays absent — a missing `fee` is not "free" and a missing `access`
    is not "anyone".
  · New `parking` kind in the taxonomy, and it is the one kind where THE PIN
    IS THE ANSWER: the question asked of a car park is "where is there one
    near here", so a pinned lot has answered it. Every other kind can be
    pinned and still say nothing.

FIX 4 — a constraint is only a constraint when there is something to
constrain. Both halves of searchcore: when eating the phrase would leave the
query empty, the words go back in and are matched as words. The filters stay
set either way. parity 528/528 comparisons, no disagreement.
Also: `access:parking` now has a column, so the page stops apologising and
says what it did — "car parks first, everything else under namesakes".

NEAR A LANDMARK NOW MEANS METRES.

  A landmark named in the query is lifted OUT of it before anything is
  matched — the same way a constraint is — and the proximity word with it.
  What remains is what the reader is looking FOR; the landmark becomes the
  point everything is measured from. 68 points, from the register the
  bearings layer already keeps, published as data/search_landmarks.json with
  the coordinate READ FROM THE RECORD, so a corrected pin moves the sort.

  ROMANISATION IS A RULE, NOT A LIST. Michael wrote "taphae". The register
  held Tha Phae, Thapae, Tha Pae, Thaphae and Tapae — and not that one.
  Chasing spellings into a list one at a time is the mistake the mined shelf
  table already taught us, so: in Thai romanisation the h after t, p and k is
  written or not written by whoever painted the sign, and so is the space.
  `th?a\s*ph?ae` covers all six spellings with one pattern.

  THE LANDMARK'S OWN RECORD GOES FIRST. Typing "tha phae gate" returned the
  gate FOURTH, behind two cafés whose road field spelled it the way the
  reader had. Nought metres beats every argument about spelling.

  A STATED CONSTRAINT NARROWS; A MINED SHELF WORD ONLY LIFTS. "motorcycle"
  hints at the transport shelf — which also holds every rental counter and
  repair hut in the city, so with a distance sort the nearest of those sat on
  top of a search whose first word was "parking". "parking" is not a hint.

  His query, then and now:

SLASH AND BURN — what came off the results page, and why each one went:

  · the ตอนนี้·ใกล้ๆ strip (events today, temperature, PM2.5). The right
    first line on every page a reader arrives at with no question. This is
    the one page they arrive at WITH one.
  · the province on every row. Two hundred repetitions of "เชียงใหม่" when
    every row is in Chiang Mai. It prints only when the rows span both.
  · "showing 200 of 3,931". The heading already says 3,931. The line keeps
    only the part the reader can act on: "add a word to narrow it" — and it
    moved BELOW the list, being advice about it.
  · "including words that mean the same thing". Fired on the ordinary
    successful search and told nobody anything they could do.
  · "wrong results? tell the ants" under every search, including the ones
    that worked. It was added as feedback AT THE POINT OF FAILURE; now it
    appears at one.
  · shelf headings under a distance sort. Grouping sorts shelves by size,
    which quietly re-sorted the rows — three cafés above the gate itself.
  · a topic card over a question it does not answer. "parking near tha phae
    gate" opened the MOAT door, nine ways to tell inside from outside, above
    the car parks. The panel is matched against what is LEFT to ask.
  · the second distance on a row. A car park is named for the landmark it
    stands nearest, so in a list measured from Tha Phae Gate a row read
    "ใกล้แจ่งก๊ะต๊ำ 290 ม. · 430 ม." — two numbers, neither obviously the
    one asked about. Under a landmark sort the row shows the base name and
    one distance.
