MOT DANG — THE MAP IS THE APPLICATION
Architecture proposal, 2026-09-07 · rev 2, same day, after Nan's reframe

Nan: "I'd like navigation and discovery to be primarily driven by map surface
UX. Text based nav is so 1999."

Nan, on being handed rev 1: "What I'm trying to do is make the same data
that's easily understood/available to robots, be equally available to humans.
Humans like to navigate complex problems about their cities via maps. They
prefer this to text search or directories. We have rich maps. I want to use
these as empathetic search tools for humans."

WO-74 · PROPOSED · nothing built, nothing deployed, no work order filed.
Every number below says where it came from.

==============================================================================
0. THE GOAL, RESTATED IN HER WORDS — AND IT IS A NUMBER
==============================================================================

Not "the map is nicer". The goal is PARITY: a person standing in Chiang Mai
should be able to ask this directory everything a machine can ask it.

Measured on the live site, 2026-09-07, by reading both doors:

  A ROBOT can ask sixteen things.  (publish/api.js, the ALLOWED list, live —
  /api/v1/health returns 200 JSON)

      q · province · cat · sub · tag · lens · facet · has · min_ants ·
      near · radius · open_now · open_at · sort · limit · offset

  A HUMAN can ask three.  (motdang.net/md.js fetched live, 233,724 chars —
  every URL parameter the site's own JavaScript reads:)

      q · near · stops

  and of those, `near` only arrives via a location tap or a landmark lifted
  out of a sentence, and `stops` is the route planner.

  Sixteen against three. The three the robot has that matter most — sub (WHICH
  KIND), radius (HOW FAR), open_at (WHEN) — have no human control anywhere on
  the site.

THE DEMONSTRATION. Your circle at Tha Phae Gate, 18.78764, 98.99357, the one
you geocoded standing there. Asked at the robot door, live, this afternoon:

    /api/v1/places?near=18.78764,98.99357&radius=300&sub=parking

    8 results, sorted by distance:
        92 m   ลานจอดรถ · ใกล้ประตูท่าแพ 120 ม.
       116 m   ที่จอดรถ · ใกล้ประตูท่าแพ 80 ม.
       129 m   ลานจอดรถ · ใกล้ประตูท่าแพ 100 ม.

Standing on that same spot and typing, you were given 810 m, and four of the
nine ways a person phrases that question never show the near ones at all
(go/wrongtool §4, go/halfhour).

**The data was never missing. The door was.** A machine asked in three
structured facts and got the right answer in 120 ms. You asked in your own
language and got the catalogue.

That is the whole project, and it means the work is not "add a map". It is:
close a sixteen-to-three gap.

==============================================================================
1. WHY THE MAP IS THE ONLY SURFACE THAT CAN CLOSE IT
==============================================================================

Ask how a TEXT interface would reach parity, honestly. It would need a control
for each of the sixteen — a kind picker, a radius field, a day-and-time picker,
a has-a-phone checkbox, a sort menu. That is an advanced-search form, and an
advanced-search form is 1999 in the worst sense: it makes the reader fill in
the machine's argument list.

A map carries the same sixteen as GESTURES, and most of them for free:

    near        the centre of the map, or the you-dot
    bbox        the viewport itself — nobody types it, panning IS typing it
    radius      a circle you drag
    province    the viewport again
    cat / sub   the chip rail
    tag/facet   the chip rail's second tier
    open_now    a lamp toggle
    open_at     a time scrubber — "9pm Thursday" as a slider
    has         a completeness toggle (only show ones with hours / a phone)
    sort        implicit on a map; explicit only in the list twin
    limit       the fold
    q           the box, kept, for naming a thing you already know

**A viewport is WHERE and HOW FAR, given continuously and without vocabulary.**
That is why the map is not a prettier front end for this site. It is the only
surface that holds the machine's whole argument list without ever showing a
person an argument list.

This gives a rule that can be a TEST, not an intention:

    EVERY PARAMETER THE API ACCEPTS HAS A NAMED HUMAN GESTURE.
    tests/test_parity.py walks publish/api.js's ALLOWED list and fails on any
    parameter with no gesture registered against it.

Written that way, parity stops being a goal and becomes a gate — which is how
everything else on this site that stayed true stayed true.

==============================================================================
2. WHAT "EMPATHETIC" MEANS, AS PROPERTIES AND NOT AS A MOOD
==============================================================================

You said empathetic search tools. Each line below is one measured failure of
the current box, turned into a property the map surface must HOLD. None is a
feature to add later; they are what the thing is.

E1 · YOU DO NOT HAVE TO KNOW THE WORD.
     The viewport already said where. The chip rail says what is here. This
     deletes an entire class of failure rather than fixing it: "moto" not
     being in the thesaurus cannot cost you the answer if you never had to
     type it. (go/wrongtool causes 1, 2 and 3, all at once.)

E2 · THE TOOL SHOWS ITS OWN REACH.
     Radius is a hidden default of 2,000 m today. You wanted 300. A circle
     you can see and drag makes HOW FAR a thing the reader owns, and it is
     the difference between 80 m and 810 m.

E3 · PRECISION IS NEVER PUNISHED.
     Saying "motorbike" must narrow the RANKING, never the SET — your own
     rule, since in Chiang Mai there is no such thing as a car park
     exclusively for cars. Structurally: a kind filters, a constraint
     re-ranks, and leftover words only ever filter within a resolved kind.

E4 · A WORD WE DID NOT UNDERSTAND IS SAID, NOT SWALLOWED.
     Struck through and visible, never silently dropped into confident
     nonsense 4.6 km away.

E5 · ABSENT IS NOT NEGATIVE.
     No hours recorded is not "closed". No fee recorded is not "free". The
     open-now lamp is ABSENT rather than dark. On a map this matters more,
     not less: an unlit pin reads as a shut door.

E6 · THE PIN CONFESSES ITS OWN PRECISION.
     2,958 pinned rows share a coordinate with another record; one Chiang Rai
     centroid carries 145. A centroid drawn like a doorway is a promise to
     someone who is about to walk there. geoPrecision is already tracked per
     field and has never been shown to a reader.

E7 · THE ANSWER IS SIZED TO THE QUESTION.
     One result is one screen. Already your rule; today it is 27 px of answer
     in a 1,484 px page.

E1 and E2 are the two that only a map can give. E3–E7 are owed to a reader on
any surface, and the map is simply where they become visible.

    it no longer blocks anything here.

==============================================================================

More of this is built than anyone would guess:

                     maxBounds/minZoom, ◎/fullscreen/scale, frame(), legible()
                     (the 11 px phone type floor), version() cache-busting, a
                     drawn-SVG fallback, and a TESTED no-basemap branch.
                     MDMAP.ready() is the only sanctioned way to paint.
  map_ground.py      the same ground rendered in Python, offline, from the
                     shipped tiles. The browser's twin.
  explore_layer.py   /map.html — 47 shelf chips, six inks at once, per-shelf

  here_layer.py      /here.html — and, quietly, THE SPATIAL INDEX: the
                     catalogue tiled into 0.01° cells, 3,364 files, 15 MB total
                     on disk (counted in docs/data/here/ today). Nine cells
                     around the old city are 166 kB gzipped (WO-64) against
                     1.5 MB for index.json. The densest single cell is 1,075
                     rows / 139 kB raw.
  MDCARD             the tap sheet every map already opens.
  WO-69's panel      a card body opens a panel IN PLACE over ONE shared
                     MapLibre mount, moved card to card.
  /api/v1/places     deployed worker over a baked index, 120 ms, already takes
                     q · cat · sub · tag · lens · facet · has · min_ants ·
                     near · radius · open_now · open_at · sort. No bbox.
  search_tables.json WO-69 put the relations in the index at last: t (tags),
                     tt (trade), st (street), ar (tambon/amphoe), hk (hours),
                     every one an int.
  the registers      68 landmarks with coordinates (WO-68) · 13 kinds
                     (data/curated/kinds.json) · zones/ย่าน · 50,946 measured
                     `near` edges (WO-69) · 127 tag slugs on 7,873 records.

Missing is not capability. Missing is that **the map is a viewer inside a page
instead of the page inside which everything else happens.** Five map surfaces
exist (/map.html, /here.html, 16,665 place maps, plan.html, toilets) and none
of them can hand a view to another.

==============================================================================
5. THE INVERSION, IN ONE SENTENCE
==============================================================================

    Today:  a text page that contains a map.
    After:  a map that opens text when you ask it something.

And the corollary that keeps the site alive while that happens:

    The text pages do not go away. They stop being the navigation and become
    the SUBSTRATE — the addressable, shareable, crawlable, LLM-readable
    statement of what the map is showing. Same data, two renderings, one
    source. map_ground.py has worked exactly this way since August.

==============================================================================
6. THE ARCHITECTURE — SEVEN LAYERS
==============================================================================

L0 · MDVIEW — ONE STATE OBJECT, AND IT IS THE URL
------------------------------------------------
State is scattered across five surfaces today: explore's hash, here's cells,
search's query string, plan's ?stops=, my.html's pins. That is why no surface
can hand off to another.

    MDVIEW = { center, zoom, bbox, kinds[], constraints{}, focus }

One object. It serializes to the hash (the format explore_layer already
publishes), it is the SOLE input to retrieval, and it is what "send this view"
ships. Every surface becomes a subscriber, not an owner. Search, plan, here,
my-map and the province maps all become projections of one view.

This layer is invisible to a reader and everything below depends on it.

L1 · RETRIEVAL IS KEYED ON A BBOX, NOT A STRING
-----------------------------------------------
The single largest change, and the cheapest, because both halves exist.

  zoom ≥ 13 → THE CELLS. Generalize docs/data/here/ from a here.html private
    asset into the site's spatial index. A phone viewport at z15 covers 4–12
    cells ≈ 60–200 kB gzipped. Immutable, content-hashed, cache-forever, works
    with no origin, and answers offline. This is P6 from go/wrongtool — "stop
    shipping the catalogue to answer one question" — and it is already built,
    just not admitted.

  zoom < 13 → THE WORKER. `bbox=` on /api/v1/places is roughly fifteen lines
    beside the near/radius maths already in publish/api.js. Above z13 the
    worker returns AGGREGATES, never points (see L2).

The rule to write down: **a reader must never download a record that is not on
their screen.** 1.5 MB before the first character of an answer is the current
number, and it is the thing that decides whether this site is pleasant on Thai
mobile data.

L2 · DENSITY IS A HIERARCHY, AND STACKS MUST CONFESS
----------------------------------------------------
You cannot draw 24,019 pins, and two facts make naive clustering dishonest:

  · 2,958 pinned rows share a coordinate with another record (WO-69) — one CR
    centroid carries 145 — because a tambon or postcode centroid stacks
    everything it placed onto one point.
  · geoPrecision is already tracked per field. The site knows which pins are
    doorways and which are centroids and has never told a reader.

So three regimes:

    z < 12    a zone/tambon polygon carrying a COUNT. "Is there anything out
              there" is a real question and a number answers it.
    z 12–14   clusters, precomputed per cell at build so they stay static.
    z ≥ 15    individual pins, and a coincident stack renders as a STACK
              MARKER that says so: "145 here · a district centroid, not a
              doorway."

A dot at a centroid drawn like a dot at a doorway is a false fact stated
2,958 times. Map-first makes that visible, so it has to be fixed first.

L3 · WHAT IS A CHIP, AND THE CHIPS COUNT WHAT IS ON SCREEN
----------------------------------------------------------
13 kinds is too coarse to be interesting; 175 shelves is far too many for a
chip row; the six-ink cap in explore_layer is correct and holds (a seventh
colour has to repeat one, and the chip carrying the ink is what makes the chip
row the legend).

The move that makes this discovery rather than filtering:

    THE CHIP RAIL IS COUNTED FROM THE CURRENT VIEWPORT, AND RE-COUNTS ON PAN.

Pan into the old city and the rail reads what is actually there —
วัด 41 · อาหาร 380 · ที่จอดรถ 47 — and a kind with nothing in view does not
render a chip at all. No words, no explanation, no empty shelf. Michael's
rule ("if you need words to explain it, you're fucking up") is satisfiable
here in a way it is not in a text nav.

Second tier, on a chip's own tap: sub-shelf, then the relations WO-69 just
put in the index (tag · street · tambon · nearest landmark). Finding BY TAG
with no words typed already works in the engine; it has never had a surface.

Beside the rail sit the other three gestures that close §0's gap, and they are
the ones no reader has today:
    · A DRAGGABLE RADIUS RING — `radius`, and E2. Default visible, not 2,000 m
      and hidden.
    · A TIME SCRUBBER — `open_at`. "Open 9pm Thursday" as a slider over the
      4,398 records that carry hours. Absent hours stay absent, never dark.
    · A COMPLETENESS TOGGLE — `has` and `min_ants`. "Only the ones we know
      something about" is a question readers ask constantly and cannot ask.

L4 · A TAP NEVER LEAVES THE MAP
-------------------------------
WO-69 built this and it should become the sitewide law. The sheet has three
detents:

    peek   name · metres · ONE deciding fact · the open-now lamp if hours
           are held (absent, never "closed", when nobody recorded any)
    half   chips (each one a filter), 📍, related-nearby from the 50,946
           measured edges, events, add-to-plan
    full   the place page's own content, in the sheet

The place page URL stays the canonical address — it is what gets shared,
indexed and answered from — but inside the map it renders in the sheet. From
go/wrongtool §3: for 68% of car parks the row already contains everything we
know, and the link is a promise of more that is not there. A sheet that opens
in place costs a reader nothing when the answer is thin.

L5 · THE LIST TWIN, AT THE SAME URL
-----------------------------------
Two hard problems map-first creates, and one mechanism solves both.

  · A crawler cannot pan. 99% of arrivals are not direct (go/arrivals);
    ~10,400 place pages are already noindex; the whole LLM-retrieval story
    runs on text pages.
  · A map is not readable for everyone. Low vision. A 1 MB MapLibre parse on
    a mid-budget Android. Thai mobile data. LINE's in-app WebView.

    Every MDVIEW renders TWO ways from one source: the map, and a list that
    states the same view in words at the same URL.

Not a fallback and not charity — the identical rendering, and it is what the
crawler, the model and the screen reader all get. map_shell's tested
no-basemap branch is the seed; map_ground.py is the proof the pattern works.
The rule: **anything the map can show, a URL must be able to state in words.**

L6 · FIRST PAINT IS NOT MAPLIBRE
--------------------------------
~1 MB of JavaScript parses before a map-first site shows anything at all, on
the phones this site is actually read on. But map_ground.py already renders
the identical ground as SVG, offline, from the shipped tiles — it is how the
printed cards work.

    Paint the SVG of the current view first. Hydrate MapLibre over it.

The reader sees the map immediately and it is the SAME map. And since you
struck "purely static" on 2026-09-07 (go/frontdoor F6), the worker may render
that first view at the edge rather than only at build.

L7 · WHAT THE TEXT NAV IS FOR AFTERWARDS
----------------------------------------
Demoted, not deleted, and to the one job it is genuinely better at: naming a
thing you already know. The box stays for "Rajavej Hospital". It stops being
the way you find out what is near you, because it is bad at it and the
viewport is good at it.

7. SEQUENCE — IN THE ORDER THAT PAYS
==============================================================================

  Ph 0  MDVIEW + bbox on the worker + promote the cells to THE index, and
        `tests/test_parity.py` — the gate from §1, red on day one, listing
        the thirteen parameters with no human gesture. Invisible to a reader.
        Everything else depends on it. Small.
  Ph 1  /map.html becomes the application: viewport-counted chips, tap→sheet,
        no navigation away.
  Ph 2  the density hierarchy + the stack confession. (Blocks Ph 1 going out
        at province zoom.)
  Ph 3  the list twin at every map URL; the homepage hop (WO-64's two
        unwired build.py lines are the same work).
  Ph 4  relations: tags as chips, the near edges in the sheet.
  Ph 5  SVG first paint, then edge render.

Ph 0–2 is the honest minimum for "map-primary". Ph 3 is what stops it costing
the site its arrivals. Ph 4 is Michael's ask. Ph 5 is what makes it pleasant
on a phone in Thailand.

==============================================================================
8. WHAT THIS COSTS THAT NOTHING ELSE HAS COST
==============================================================================

Stated plainly because it is real:

1. **2,116 records have no pin**, and per §3 they are now future value: they
   stay crawlable and they do not shape the navigation. The cost is simply
   named rather than solved — a map-primary site cannot reach them, and every
   one that gets a pin is a record that comes back. Pinning is now the single
   highest-leverage enrichment on this site, and it is separate work.
2. **2,958 stacked pins get much more visible** when the map is the product.
   L2 addresses it honestly rather than hiding it, but honest is still ugly.
3. **The weight floor goes UP, not down**, unless Ph 5 lands. Cells make the
   DATA small; MapLibre makes the CODE big. Net win only with the SVG paint.
4. **A map cannot be crawled or quoted.** L5 is not optional if the arrivals
   work is to survive. If L5 slips, the site quietly stops being findable
   while looking better than ever.
5. **The corpus is thin behind the pin.** Parking is the one kind where the
   pin IS the answer. For a restaurant, a map that puts you within 10 m of a
   place we know nothing else about is a prettier version of the same gap.
