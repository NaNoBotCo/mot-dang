THE NEIGHBOUR TEST — THE MOST INTERESTING NUMBER IN THIS FILE
-------------------------------------------------------------------
A Foursquare row standing within REVIEW_M of a record we ALREADY HOLD ON
THE SAME SHELF is treated as that record under a different name, and
refused. The rule, and its 60 m default, came from the Overture importer.

But Overture ran against a 25,914-record catalogue. It now runs against
88,161. The city is blanketed: on a Nimman block there is a cafe of ours
within 60 m of almost any point, so the test that protected us from
duplicates in August is now refusing genuinely different shops in
September. A RULE THAT WAS RIGHT AT ONE CORPUS SIZE IS WRONG AT ANOTHER,
and nothing was watching for that.

They are not discarded. Every one is named, with the record it stands
beside and the distance, in:
    cache/foursquare_review_cm.txt
    cache/foursquare_review_cr.txt

THE RADIUS IS DOING MOST OF THE WORK, measured:

    REVIEW_M = 60 m    7,399 new records     refused 20,627
    REVIEW_M = 30 m   11,536 new records     refused 16,468
    REVIEW_M = 15 m   18,060 new records      refused 9,909

THE CURVE NEVER FLATTENS. Every halving of the circle finds thousands
more shops, so there is no radius where the count settles and says "this
is the right one". That is the finding, and it is bigger than Foursquare:
DISTANCE ALONE CANNOT TELL TWO SHOPS APART IN A DENSE CITY. The 60 m
default was not a boundary effect, it was the single biggest filter in
the pipeline, inherited from a corpus a third this size without anyone
re-deriving it.

LEFT AT 30 m — the radius import_fixtures.py measured its ATM joins at,
and a defensible middle, but still a guess. The real fix is a
name-similarity test INSIDE the circle: two coffee shops 20 m apart with
unrelated names are two coffee shops, and one shop under two spellings is
one shop. PyThaiNLP's cross-script soundex (go/tools 2.6) is the
blocking key that would do it. NOT BUILT — written down so it stays a
defect with a number rather than a setting nobody questions.

THE 210,241 WITH NO POSTCODE
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Not imported, and not thrown away — they are in the cache. A row with no
postcode cannot be put in a province without guessing, and guessing is
what put a school 139 km out in the geocoder audit on 9/5.

The pass that claims them is the tambon POLYGONS from the tools survey
(go/tools item 3.2): point-in-polygon every row, and the province stops
being a guess. That is the same work that fixes 2,116 unpinned records.
One change, two payoffs.


-------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE IMPORTER DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO
-------------------------------------------------------------------
IT DOES NOT ENRICH. Measured: against records we already hold Foursquare
would add 307 phone numbers. Overture added 3,631. Foursquare carries a
phone on 7.5% of its own northern rows; Overture on 82%, because Overture
is Facebook business pages and Facebook is where a Thai shop writes its
number. So `apply()` returns enriched=0 always and never touches a field
on a record we hold. A side benefit: no existing record's give-back status
can be changed by this importer at all.

IT DOES NOT MAP EVERY CATEGORY. 7,330 rows reach no shelf. Every unmapped
category is counted and named in cache/foursquare_unmapped_{cm,cr}.txt, so
a shelf that should exist and does not is VISIBLE rather than silent —
the lesson from the KNOWN_EMPTY beauty shelves. Seven categories are
refused on purpose with a reason each, written into
data/curated/foursquare_categories.json: residential buildings, offices,
housing developments, structures, roads, municipalities, government
buildings. An office is not a trade; a block of flats is not a lookup.


-------------------------------------------------------------------
ON FORBIDDEN_VIA, BECAUSE NAN ASKED WHETHER I INVENTED IT
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Her question, 2026-09-08: "What is a forbidden give back list? I thought
we decided everything was permitted? Did you sneak in a new rule?"

No. And the two things are opposite directions.

WHAT MAY COME IN is wide open, and she widened it again the same day:
"we should be aggressively using share-alike." Nothing blocks ingestion.
Foursquare came in today under Apache-2.0.

FORBIDDEN_VIA governs only what may be UPLOADED TO OPENSTREETMAP. OSM's
own licence requires contributed data be free of third-party rights, so
pushing a commercial directory's rows into OSM is barred by OSM, not by
us. The list (weed.th, yellowpages, cmhy, google, tripadvisor, wongnai,
gowabi, agoda, booking.com, foursquare) is that constraint written down.

Provenance check, per the standing rule: `provenance.py` is UNTRACKED in
git, so `git log -S` cannot be run on it. What it serves is
notes/osm-giveback-licence-gate-2026-09-05.txt, whose first lines record
HER decision that day: "BROAD — contribute everything licence-clean."
The list exists to execute that decision, not to narrow it.

ONE LINE IN IT IS NOT HERS AND SHOULD BE FLAGGED: the comment says "Add
to it freely; removing from it needs a reason in writing." Nobody asked
her for that asymmetry. It is a past session's caution wearing a rule's
clothes. Treat the list as editable in both directions.

Cost of today's ingest against give-back: zero. These records were never
giveable, and because provenance is per-field they cannot make an
existing record less giveable.
