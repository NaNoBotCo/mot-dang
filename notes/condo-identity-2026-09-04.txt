Two halves of one identity, and nothing joins them. The map knows where the
building stands and not what it is called in Thai; the register knows the Thai
name, the tambon and what the Treasury assesses it at, and not where it is.
A resident who types either half is answered by a page that does not look like
his building — no pin on one side, no valuation or Thai name on the other.

This is not a Punna problem. 185 of 353 mapped Chiang Mai buildings carry
no Thai name at all; the register writes every one of its 373 rows in Thai.
The join written in WO-27 is exact-name-only and correctly so, but it is blind
by construction to every building whose two names are the same word in two
scripts.


What was built
--------------

The loanword tier — import_condo_register.loanword_candidates(). The
register's Thai is read by RTGS (translit.py), the generic words are stripped
(residence, condo, mansion, @, the district), and what remains is compared as a
CONSONANT SKELETON: ปันนา → Panna → pn, Punna → pn. Building numbers are
compared separately and reported, never assumed. 27 candidates in Chiang Mai
and none in Chiang Rai, written to cache/condo_register_review_<prov>.txt beside the
existing near-miss list, with the merges.json line to paste.

Nothing is merged by the machine, and the floor is set by two facts. The
skeleton is a coarse instrument: over the whole catalogue it offered เฮือนป้อ
เลี้ยง for Wat Luang, Doi Lang and a music shop. Confining the Latin side to
the housing shelf is what makes a two-consonant floor safe — and a two-
consonant floor is required, because Punna itself reduces to pn and a higher
one missed the building this work order started with.

Six pairs settled by a person (name-for-name, unique, no number in doubt):
Ban Haw Kham · Pansook The Urban · Palm Springs Nimman Areca · The Star Hill ·
Mountain Front · Mountain View. Each map record kept its pin and its Latin
name and gained the Treasury's spread.

Punna itself is NOT settled, deliberately. Three footprints, five register
rows, and no evidence on this disk saying which footprint is Punna 1. Merging
on a guess puts one building's valuation on another building — the error the
whole file is written around. It sits in the candidate list as
number unconfirmed, where a resident or a walk settles it.

A register building is a condominium — settle_sub(), after the merges.
The register lists buildings registered under the Condominium Act B.E. 2522,
and that registration is what makes a building an อาคารชุด;
building=apartments describes form, not tenure. 7 buildings re-filed; the CM
condo shelf 354 → 408.

The facts the site has held and never printed. 362 records carry the
Treasury's assessed spread and 109 carry a complex key; no place page showed
either. Both now render in the facts list: the spread with the sentence that
says it is the transfer-fee basis and not a market price, and the complex as
links to the sibling buildings — which is the only way a page can distinguish
One Plus Suan Dok 1 from the other ten rows behind one map footprint.

"Is this a holistic fix?" — no, and here is what the honest one was
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Nan asked that of the curated-unions plan, and the answer was no. A file of
confirmed spelling unions fixes the condo shelf and leaves the disease. So the
disease was measured across all 21,937 records:

| source | records | Thai-only name | no pin |
|---|---|---|---|
| ONAB temple register | 2,084 | 100% | 943 |
| OBEC schools | 1,302 | 100% | 6 |
| FDA pharmacies | 907 | 98% | 99 |
| MOPH health | 490 | 100% | 0 |
| condominium register | 367 | 98% | 367 |
| OPEC private schools | 168 | 100% | 168 |
| OpenStreetMap | 14,851 | — 58% Latin-only | — |

Only 24% of the catalogue holds both scripts of its own name. Every state
register writes Thai; the open map mostly writes Latin; so any place that
reaches this site down both paths stands in it twice under two alphabets.
Punna is not a condominium problem. It is the shape of the whole catalogue,
and it was found on the condo shelf because that is where a resident looked.

So the fix went to the reading, not to a matching rule. translit.reading
is what the search index already matches on and what every Thai-only row shows
as its romanisation. It was getting these names wrong:

    ยูนีค          Yu Nik      → Unique
    ฮิลล์ไซด์       Hinsai      → Hillside
    ทรีบูทีค        Sibuthik    → Tree Boutique
    ไฮแลนด์         Hailaen     → Highland
    แฮปปี้          Papi        → Happy
    นอร์ท           Not         → North
    ปันนา           Panna       → Punna

Twenty building loanwords went into data/curated/rtgs_lexicon.json, which
already existed for exactly this (บิ้วตี้ is Beauty, not Bioti), plus a new
brand section for a name's own Latin spelling read off a record in this
same catalogue — ปันนา is Punna because three buildings on this shelf spell
it that way on their own signs. No outside source, no claim about ownership.

With the readings right, no similarity test is needed at all. Families
join on the exact romanised token: 57 families of two or more on the housing
shelf, 22 of them holding a register row and a mapped building together,
Punna's ten among them. Every fuzzy rule was measured and refused; the
audit script still prints what each would have joined, and applies none.

This reaches further than the condo shelf, because the reading is used
everywhere: the search index's matched-never-shown alias, the romanisation
under every Thai-only name, and any future register join.

What a family may be keyed on. data/curated/name_families.json holds the
refusal list — 30 words that name a KIND (หญิง the sex of a dormitory, อาคาร
the word building, แฟลต, เจ้าหน้าที่), a DESCRIPTOR any building may wear
(hill, town, garden, square), or a PLACE (ช้างคลาน and ช้างเผือก are two
districts, not one family). Each refusal carries its ground, so the next
person can disagree with a reason instead of a silence. Without it the shelf
grouped thirteen unrelated หอ N หญิง as a family.


The sweep, run across every shelf
---------------------------------

Nan: "do the sweep everywhere." The instrument is
importers/audit_readings.py, and the evidence is the catalogue's own:
4,987 records hold a Thai name AND a human English name, so where our
reading disagrees with the sign, the sign is right and the disagreement is a
bug report somebody already wrote down.

Only pairs that share a CONSONANT SHAPE were taken. That is the line between
a transliteration and a translation, and it is the whole discipline: กาแฟ →
"Coffee" and เภสัช → "Pharmacy" are translations, the readings *Kafae and
Phesat are correct, and a translation belongs in the search index's
matched-never-shown words, never in a romanisation.

Twenty-two loanwords and sixteen brand spellings went in. The chains are
the finding a reader would feel first:

Not one of the twenty Watsons or thirteen Boots carries an English name in
this catalogue. A reader typing the word on the shopfront was told the
directory did not have it — the same failure as Punna, on a shelf where the
reader is usually looking for a pharmacy tonight.

Measured over the whole corpus:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| readings that match the sign exactly | 1,360 of 4,987 (27.3%) | 1,495 (30.0%) |
| Thai-only records with no English name whose romanisation now carries the English word | — | 490 |

Those 490 are 407 medical, 382 essentials (the pharmacies), 36 housing, 9
schools. They are findable in English for the first time, because the
romanisation is what the search index matches on.

What the temple sweep found instead. On the wat shelf the readings are
mostly right — Wat, Phra That, Doi, Luang all correct — because those are Thai
words and RTGS is built for them. What is wrong there is different in kind:
วัดสุวรรณ reads Wat Suoraron and วัดป่ายาง reads Wat Painga, which are
segmentation faults in Pali compounds, not missing vocabulary. They belong to
irregular and to the segmenter, and they are NOT fixed here — naming them is
the honest end of this sweep, not a claim to have finished them.


One thing the sweep found in the lexicon itself
-----------------------------------------------

The boundary this work order states — a reading romanises, it does not
translate — is not currently held by the file it is stated in. The lexicon
already translates where it should transliterate: สาขา comes back as branch,
ชุมชน as Community, โรงเรียน as School, วิทยาลัย as College. So
โรงเรียนนานาชาติเชียงใหม่ reads "Nanachati School Chiang Mai" — half read,
half translated. Those entries predate this sweep and nothing here changed
them: they may well be a deliberate kindness to an English reader, and
undoing them would move thousands of readings on the schools shelf. It is
recorded because the next person to add a word will look at those entries to
learn the rule, and they teach the opposite of it. Somebody should decide
which way the file goes.
