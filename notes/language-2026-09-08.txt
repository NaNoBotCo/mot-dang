THE LANGUAGE TOGGLE, AND THE WORDS IT COULD NEVER REACH
WO-77 · 2026-09-08 · zero-network, built, NOT deployed


WHAT YOU AND MICHAEL SAID

  "The language toggle on motdang.net fails a lot. And there seem to be a
   bunch of loose terms presented only in one script, which detracts from
   accessibility and removes opportunity for humans to see patterns and infer
   meaning."

Both halves are real and they are different faults. Measured before anything
was changed, on the live site and on the catalogue on disk.


PART ONE — WHY THE TOGGLE FAILED

  1. IT WAS APPLIED AFTER THE PAGE HAD ALREADY BEEN DRAWN.

     Every page shipped with a bare <body> and no language on it. The choice
     you had stored was read and applied by md.js — 248 KB, and the LAST tag
     on the page, after everything else had painted. So a reader who had
     picked English got a fully drawn bilingual page first and the switch
     afterwards, on every single click. On a 1.5 Mbps connection that is
     seconds of the wrong language, every time.

     From the outside that is indistinguishable from a toggle that failed.

     FIXED: the language now goes on <html>, written by a 180-byte inline
     script in the head, before the first pixel. The stylesheet's twenty-nine
     `body.lang-*` rules moved with it. md.js still handles the click; it is
     no longer what decides what you see when the page opens.

     A second gain fell out of it: the served page now carries lang-both, so a
     reader with scripts off gets both languages instead of Thai alone.

  2. A LOT OF THE PAGE WAS NOT MARKED WITH A LANGUAGE AT ALL.

     Across 59 live pages sampled at random: 408 runs of Thai and 966 runs of
     Latin sat outside any language span. The toggle cannot hide what is not
     marked, so those showed identically in all three modes. On a place page
     they were concentrated in one place, and it is the finding of this note:

       THE LABELS WERE BILINGUAL. THE VALUES WERE NOT.

     หมวด · Category, นิกาย · Nikaya, ระดับชั้นที่เปิดสอน · Levels taught —
     every field label on the site has been in both languages for months. The
     value printed beside it was Thai alone. A reader without Thai was told
     what they were looking at and never what it said.

  3. "API · API". bi("API","API") printed the same word twice, in both
     languages, because nothing checked whether the pair was a pair. Same for
     LINE. Fixed inside bi() itself, so it holds everywhere.

  Also paired, each of which was on every page on the site: the copy-link
  button (Thai only), the sponsor label (both languages in one unmarked
  string), the อ่านง่าย button (a bilingual aria-label and a Thai-only face —
  an English reader saw an unexplained button), the "มดแดง" map link, and the
  พ.ศ. edition date on 1,371 temple records, which now carries its CE year.


PART TWO — THE TERMS THAT EXIST IN ONE SCRIPT

  THE CENSUS, over all 88,161 records:

      55,472  records whose name is Thai with no English name
      36,442  addresses with no Latin character anywhere in them
      33,369  attribute values printing in Thai alone, over 59 fields

  The romaniser already reaches a place's NAME — that was WO-72, and on the
  sample 189 of 196 Thai-only headings carry a reading. It reached nothing
  else. The fields were where the silence was.

  WHAT WAS BUILT, AND WHY IT COMPOSES RATHER THAN LISTS

  `terms.py` + `data/curated/thai_terms.json`. The registers write in closed
  vocabularies: `registerUses` has 39 distinct values on 559 records and every
  one of them is the same dozen words joined with slashes; `levels` has 35 and
  they are six stage words and a number. A flat list of 39 strings is 39
  chances to have missed one. A table of twelve words that COMPOSE covers the
  fortieth value the day the register adds it — and says nothing at all when
  it cannot, which is the part that makes it trustworthy.

  Coverage of what the pages print, measured:

      นิกาย · Nikaya                  1,717 / 1,717   100%
      ประเภทวัด · Standing            1,717 / 1,717   100%
      วิสุงคามสีมา · Wisung           1,717 / 1,717   100%
      ระดับชั้น · Levels taught       1,516 / 1,516   100%
      สังกัดเขตพื้นที่ · Education area 1,302 / 1,302   100%
      ประเภทตามใบอนุญาต · Licensed as   243 /   243   100%
      ผู้รับใบอนุญาต · Licence held by  196 /   196   100%
      ที่อยู่ · Address              34,257 / 36,442    94%

  So a temple page now reads

      นิกาย · Nikaya       มหานิกาย — Maha Nikaya, the larger monastic order
      ประเภทวัด · Standing  วัดราษฎร์ — a people's temple, not a royal one
      ที่อยู่ · Address     ตำบลหนองบัว อำเภอไชยปราการ จังหวัดเชียงใหม่
                           Tambon Nong Bua · Amphoe Chai Prakan · Changwat
                           Chiang Mai

  and a school page says อ.2–ป.6 — Kindergarten 2 to Grade 6, which is the
  fact a parent came for. ป.6 is Grade 6 and ม.3 is Grade 9, and getting that
  offset wrong would send someone to the wrong school, so it is a fixed case
  in the test rather than a phrase somebody can tidy.

  THREE REFUSALS, each deliberate

  1. A GLOSS BEATS A READING, and the first draft printed both. "ได้รับ ·
     Draiba · granted" spends a word on a reading that is neither useful nor,
     as it happens, correct. A reading is for a NAME — something you say
     aloud, type into a search, or show a driver. On an administrative term
     it is clutter on top of the answer. Where the meaning is known the
     meaning prints alone; where it is not, the reading is all there is and
     it prints.

  2. A HALF-GLOSS IS REFUSED ENTIRELY. If one Thai word in a value is unknown,
     the whole value comes back in Thai. "residential unit · ??? · balcony
     area" reads as though the site understood the row. The Thai alone is at
     least true.

  3. NOTHING TRANSLATES A NAME. A place's English name is a sourced field and
     stays one. Where a head word is a dictionary word and the rest is a name
     — ลานจอดรถ มหาวิทยาลัยเชียงใหม่ — the head is glossed and the name is
     READ: "parking yard Chiang Mai University".

  THE ADMIN LEXICON GREW 59 → 403. `build_trade_lexicon.py` had been reading
  only the curated admin file, which holds 43 อำเภอ and 16 ตำบล — so of the
  294 distinct ตำบล the catalogue prints on 3,031 records it could read 8%.
  It now reads the catalogue's own admin names too. Amphoe are still spelt
  the way the road signs spell them, because the curated file is still read
  first (กัลยาณิวัฒนา is Galyani Vadhana, not Kanlayani Watthana).

  THE SOURCE CREDITS, 13 names on 4,662 records, now carry the English the
  agency itself publishes: ทะเบียนวัด สำนักงานพระพุทธศาสนาแห่งชาติ · Temple
  register, National Office of Buddhism (ONAB). The credit is how a reader
  weighs a row — a register outranks a directory outranks a guess — and it
  was legible to half the readership.

  THE FRONT PAGE. The horoscope chips (29 of them, single Thai letters), the
  currency names and the day and zodiac names all had a bilingual aria-label
  and a Thai-only face. They now carry both, under your own P2 rule from
  7 September — the English half stands down in ไทย+EN mode and appears only
  for a reader who has chosen English, so the row does not get longer.


WHAT IT MEASURES

  A place page, freshly rendered against the real catalogue, in English-only
  mode — runs of Thai the toggle cannot hide:

      before   6.9 per page
      after    3.3 per page, and 3.0 of those are the masthead: the มดแดง
               logo and the two toggle buttons themselves, which have to show
               in every mode or you cannot find your way back.

  Bare Thai values in a <dd>: 300 across 200 built pages before, 0 across 30
  freshly rendered pages after.

  Two new suites: `tests/test_terms.py` (28 checks, including the refusals and

F2–F5, EXECUTED (Nan, same day: "leave the cinema stuff alone… Execute F2-F5")

  F1 stands. The cinema listings are untouched.

  F2 — THE LISTING SHEET now has the same fix. `th.py` carries the toggle for
     all three of its pages and had the identical fault: the class on <body>,
     written at the foot of the page. Moved to <html>, a `LANG_PREPAINT`
     script added to each of the three heads, `class="lang-both"` served, and
     the seven `body.lang-*` selectors across th.py and sheet.py repointed.
     Rebuilt; all twelve of its suites green, and the built index.html and
     add.html carry zero `body.lang` and the pre-paint script.

  F3 — THE READING, WHERE IT WAS NOT.

     Two of the three places named on the first pass turned out to be already
     done and are worth recording as such: SHELF PAGES carry 1,209 readings on
     the live wat shelf, and SEARCH RESULT ROWS carry 199 on a 205-row search.
     Both came in with WO-72. The note was repeating a measurement from 9/6.

     What was actually wrong on the search page is the same fault as the place
     page, one level down: **the result row's name and its reading were both
     unmarked**. The name showed in every mode by accident rather than by the
     `.solo` rule, and the READING showed in Thai-only mode too — an English
     transcription printed for a reader who had asked for Thai alone. The name
     now goes through a new `mdSolo()`, the client twin of `name_bi()`, and
     the reading and the English name ride in the English slot where they
     belong. `mdBi()` also learnt the same collapse `bi()` learnt, so the
     client cannot hydrate "API · API" either.

     And the shelf words on those rows: `MD_CATWORDS` welds "th · en" into one
     string, which is right for the search haystack and wrong on a chip — so
     every result row printed both languages whichever one you had picked.
     A 23-entry `MD_CATPAIR` now supplies the pair for the three places a
     shelf word is SHOWN; the haystack is untouched.

     The front page's own remainder, all of it Thai with English sitting
     unused in the same record: the event slides printed the venue as one
     welded string and now carry the pair as markup; the day of the week and
     the zodiac year in the fortune tile were written as PLAIN TEXT by the
     live updater, so moving the date put the Thai back whichever language you
     had chosen; and the gold price's source line.

  F4 — THE TWO BAD READINGS, and a third found under them.

     แก้ว read "Kwae" — the ว taken as the second half of a กว cluster instead
     of the final /o/ — and นวรัฐ read "Norat", the unwritten vowel between
     น and ว dropped. So ถ.แก้วนวรัฐ, a road you can see from Nawarat Bridge,
     read "Kwaenorat". ได้รับ read "Draiba": ดร is a LOANWORD cluster and this
     is two words meeting, ได้ + รับ.

     Both classes are shapes the letter rules cannot decide — แคว really is
     Khwae and แก้ว really is Kaeo, and nothing in the spelling separates them
     — so the four entries went into `rtgs_lexicon.json`, which is what that
     file is for. One rule did change: the "a leading vowel makes the pair a
     cluster" exception now applies to NATIVE clusters only, because ไดร- is
     not a shape Thai builds.

     **The corpus floor moved with it: exact agreement against the
     catalogue's own human-written English names 46.9% → 47.6%, close
     70.4% → 70.7%.** ห้วยแก้ว, ต้นแก้ว and แก้วเจ้าจอม all read correctly now,
     and เบรก is still Brek.

  F5 — THE CONDOMINIUM REGISTER'S OWN WORDS now print. 559 records have
     carried `registerUses` since WO-27 and no page showed it, while the
     assessed value beside it printed all along — so the page said what a
     square metre is worth and not whether the unit may be lived in. New row,
     ตามทะเบียนใช้เป็น · Registered use, glossed by the table built this
     morning: "ห้องชุดพักอาศัย/พื้นที่ระเบียง — residential unit · balcony
     area". One bug fell out of it and is fixed: the register says the same
     thing twice — ห้องชุดเพื่อประกอบการค้า(ห้องชุดพาณิชยกรรม) — and the
     gloss was repeating it. It says it once.
