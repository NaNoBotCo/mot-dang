WHY EVERY PAGE UPLOADS EVERY DAY

  deploy.py compares checksums, so an unchanged file costs nothing: the
  62,747 .json siblings, which carry neither a date nor a hash, were skipped.
  Every .html uploaded, because every .html changed. A place page diffed
  against its live copy before the sync reached it: 42 changed lines, none
  about the place —
  1. the footer: "ปรับปรุง 2026-09-10 · updated 2026-09-10" is on 96,337 of
     96,369 pages. A new day is a new byte in every page, 6.8 GB up.
     standing_walk.sh rebuilds and republishes every 20 h for no reason but
     to keep that date current (MAX_AGE_HOURS=20, "keep the date true"). The
     line it keeps true, อัปเดตทุกวัน, arrived 8/1 in the commit "Port the
     Claude Design look" and is no longer on the home page.
  2. five ?v= hashes in every <head> (style.css, md.js, live.js, alpha.js,
     refine.js). Any CSS or JS edit changes every page. Tonight: style.css.
  3. favicon and logo lines: tonight only.

WHY THE UPLOAD ITSELF IS SLOW

  Uplink measured at 16 MB/s. The sync ran at ~1.9 MB/s, 27 files/s: one
  round trip to the bucket is ~1 s from here, each file costs a PUT and a
  verification HEAD, 32 run at once. The line was 12% busy. The bucket is
  already in APAC.

WHY THE BUILD IS SLOW

  a. The two place-page loops (build.py:16856) render one record at a time,
     pure per-record work, on one core of eight.
  b. Tag pages: 80 min for 6,220 pages. A broad tag renders every member as
     a full row: has-facebook.html is 68 MB and 48,988 rows, has-phone.html
     61 MB; 25 pages over 5 MB. Shelves split past a row count (build.py:
     "a shelf bigger than this cannot be one page"); tag pages never split.
     The place page I checked links to no has-* page.
  c. ~18 KB of inline icon sprite (<defs> i-ant… i-wat…) is byte-identical
     in every page. 60% of a 60 KB place page is SVG; the text is 10 KB.
     18 KB × 96k pages = 1.7 GB of the site, and of every full upload.

WHAT SHORTENS IT, ranked by minutes per unit of work

  ENGINEERING ONLY — no reader sees a difference
  1. deploy.py: --transfers 96 --s3-no-head. One round trip per file
     instead of two, three times as many in flight. A full re-upload in
     10–20 min instead of 60 if R2 accepts the concurrency. Two lines.
     Worst case: throttling; back off to 64. The next sync's checksum pass
     verifies what the skipped HEAD would have.
  2. build.py: fork the place-page loops across cores. ~70 min off.
     Worst case: a worker dies and leaves a short tree; publish/safety.py
     already refuses that tree.
  3. Build into docs.next/ and swap at the end (the 9/8 outage's open root
     fix). Saves no minutes alone; a build and a publish stop excluding each
     other, and a killed build never leaves a half tree at the published
     path. Needs ~8 GB free for the swap; 24 GB free tonight.

  YOURS — changes what a reader sees or what the site says
  4. The footer date. (a) drop it from place pages, keep it on the home
     page, sitemap, RSS; (b) print the record's own last-changed date, which
     moves only when the place moves; (c) keep it. With (a) or (b) a quiet
     day publishes in minutes, the forced 20-hour rebuild goes, and a data
     day uploads only the pages that changed. Worst case (a): a reader
     cannot see how old a page is. (b): they can, per place.
  5. The ?v= hashes. Serve css/js like html (max-age=0, must-revalidate, in
     worker.js cacheFor) and drop the hash from pages. Cost to a reader:
     five 304 round trips per visit, bodies cached, ~0.5 s on 3G. Gain: a
     CSS edit stops being a 6.8 GB upload. Or keep the hashes and accept the
     full upload on asset days.
  6. The icon sprite as one file (icons.svg, <use href="…#i-wat">; every
     current browser). Each place page −18 KB (−30%), the site −1.7 GB,
     every full upload a third shorter, every reader a third less HTML.
     Worst case: one more cached request on first visit.
  7. Tag pages: split the broad ones the way shelves split, or stop
     rendering has-* as pages (they are search filters). −0.4 GB and most
     of the 80-minute tail. Worst case: a link on a tag index moves.

  THE STRUCTURAL ONE — "20 minutes tops" even on a shell-change day
  8. Bodies in the bucket, shell in the Worker. The build writes the ~25 KB
     that is the place; worker.js wraps it in head, nav and footer at
     request time, cached at the edge. A shell change becomes one Worker
     deploy instead of 96k uploads; pages halve again. Cost: docs/ stops
     holding whole pages, so the gate, the tests and the HF static export
     must assemble first; the Worker gains a template. A week, not an
     afternoon.

WHAT WAS DONE, 22:35 THAT NIGHT

LIVE NOW — the Worker, deployed twice through the tower's own queue

  css, js and svg serve as  max-age=0, stale-while-revalidate=604800
  instead of a seven-day cache. That is what makes an un-fingerprinted URL
  safe, so it had to go out BEFORE any page that drops its ?v=. It is out.
  Brotli and everything else on the site are untouched; a place page, the
  front page, /api/v1/places, a 404 and a tile range all still answer.

MEASURED AND REJECTED — an edge cache for the big bodies

  Built it, deployed it, measured it against the live site, took it out
  again, and wrote the reason into worker.js so nobody builds it twice:
  serving data/index.json and vendor/ out of the colo cache keyed by etag,
  with a gzip twin baked beside each. It was SLOWER — 5.7-8.2 s where the
  plain path had been 1.8-3.5 s — and most requests missed the cache
  anyway. Cloudflare was already caching these AND compressing them with
  brotli, which beats the gzip twin; on top of that, finding the etag cost
  an extra R2 head() before every read, and filling the cache cost a
  second full read of the same 9.4 MB. Taking a body away from the CDN to
  hand-cache it is a losing trade. Site is back to its old numbers.

STAGED, NOT SHIPPED — five build.py changes, no complete build yet
