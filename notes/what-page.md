# what.html — empathy map and wording plan

Drawn 2026-08-29, the night the page was first built. The page is the answer
to one message: someone pastes a Mot Dang link into a chat and the reply comes
back "เว็บอะไรอ่ะ" — what site is this? what.html is what you send next.
So the reader is never someone browsing; it is always someone *sent*.

## Who receives the link

**1. เพื่อนใน LINE — the friend in the chat.** Thai, in town, on a phone,
mid-conversation. Gives it twenty seconds standing in a queue.
*Thinks*: probably another app that wants something from me.
*Feels*: curious but braced.
**The page must** show rather than argue, scroll light, and be over before
the queue moves. Pictures carry it; the words are the friend leaning over
saying "ดูอันนี้" — look at this one.

**2. ลูกสาวเจ้าของร้าน — the shopkeeper's daughter.** Someone said "your
shop is on this site." Arrives looking for the catch.
**The page must** put ฟรี in a picture, not a policy — the claim plate is
hers, and the creed line about money buying only the labelled box is read
with her eyes first.

**3. ฝรั่งอยู่ยาว — the resident farang.** Asks "so is this better than
Google Maps?" Will not read Thai but sees that Thai comes first everywhere,
and that is itself the answer.
**The page must** let the English halves stand as real sentences — not
glosses of the Thai — and let the refusals (no ranking, no pay-to-win)
land without a single marketing word.

**4. คนอ่านแบบตั้งใจ — the pitch reader.** A Pim, a Stan, an editor. Reads
the whole thing twice, checks one claim.
**The page must** keep every claim checkable — each plate links to the live
page it photographs, and the screenshot date is printed, because a claim a
reader can hold up against the real thing is the site's whole trick.

**5. เครื่องอ่าน — the machine.** The site is deliberately legible to
crawlers and models. The description line answers "what is Mot Dang" in
both languages, plainly, so the summary a machine gives is one we wrote.

## What "a wee bit off" meant, and the correction

The first draft wrote captions like ad copy: "One box. Big buttons." —
staccato fragments, the cadence of a brochure. The site has never once
spoken that way. Its voice is long, patient, concrete sentences with the
wink at the end of the sentence, not in place of it: "Two branches of the
same chain are not the same shop." The correction, as rules:

- Full sentences, Thai natural-first (spoken Thai, เจ้า where the site
  already uses it), English written fresh, never a translation of the Thai.
- The ants are characters who do physical work: they walk, count, read
  signs, keep boards. Cute = the totem working, never an exclamation mark.
- Numbers are the charm. "All 462, including 34 that stand within sight of
  their own twin" is funnier than any adjective.
- Recognition beats decoration: day colours, wan phra, เถลิงศก named
  plainly, the way a local would, never explained like an exhibit.
- Honesty as interface: date the screenshots, admit the page will age,
  link every plate to the thing it photographs.

## The shape

Lead (people ask what this is; the straightest answer is to point) →
six things the ants hold to (the refusals, each a full warm sentence) →
fifteen plates, each a photograph of a live page with one line under it,
tappable through to the page itself → the screenshot date, read-on links
(why, who), share block with QR.

Plates are rephotographed by hand when the furniture changes; the date
line is the debt that forces it.

## Correction, same night — the ant speaks for herself

Nan read the first wording and said it was a wee bit off to a human, then
named the fix exactly: not passive, not third person — "we did this, we did
that. write in the voice of an ant? a cute and lovely ant?" So the whole
page moved into first person plural, เรา, the ants speaking. The site
already says บอกมดแดง and ถามมด on every page; the page that answers
"what is this?" should sound like the ones being asked. The cute is in the
work, not in exclamation marks: "We checked." · "We double-checked those."
· "Counting is the thing we are best at." · "…because the person who
feeds us keeps clocks, and it rubbed off."
