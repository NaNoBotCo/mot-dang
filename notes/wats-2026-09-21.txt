THE FOUR THINGS THAT WERE WRONG

1.  NOT ALL OF THEM WERE WATS. Five stops on the published rounds were not:
    เทพมณเฑียร, which is a Hindu temple · พุทธสถานเชียงใหม่, a hall ·
    ศาลเจ้าพ่อหลักเมืองแจ่งกระต๊ำ, a shrine · สมาคมพุทธภาวนาบุญสถาน, an
    association · and a node named only "Buddhist temple". The catalogue's wat
    shelf is wider than the word — under it, inside the city, sit 41 things
    that are not wats, a Christian hall and an apron shop among them.
    A stop now has to be named as a wat, วัด or Wat at the head of its own
    name, and nothing else lets a place on.

2.  SOME WERE NOWHERE NEAR WHERE THE PAGE PUT THEM. 55 records inside the
    city come from the temple register, which pins a wat at the middle of its
    tambon: 37 of them on one point off Chotana Road, and they are wats in
    แม่อาย and ฝาง, 100 km away. Another source is exact and loose — its
    วัดดวงดี is a kilometre from the วัดดวงดี on Ratchadamnoen. Stops now come
    from the map, which is also what the road data was cut from.

3.  WAT PHRA SINGH WAS ON NO ROUND AT ALL. Nor Wat Suan Dok, nor วัดดวงดี.
    Their pins land nearest a footpath inside their own compound that joins
    nothing — sixteen junctions, in Phra Singh's case — so the router called
    them unreachable and set them aside. A stop is now snapped to the
    connected city. Every one of the 92 is reachable; none is set aside.

4.  THE LINES WENT THROUGH BUILDINGS. The map joined the stops with straight
    lines and said so in the caption. Every leg is now drawn along the road it
    is walked on, with the walk in from each gate to the street drawn as a
    grey stub — the same way /plan.html has always drawn it.

    And the order: nine stops is small enough to solve exactly, so each round
    is the shortest closed walk rather than a good guess at it.
