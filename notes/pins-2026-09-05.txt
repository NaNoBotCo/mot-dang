THREE DEFECTS IN A GEOCODER THAT WAS NEVER RUN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------


importers/geocode_local.py was built, documented and measured, wired into
import_opendata.py and import_weedth.py — and never once pointed at the 2,116
records already on disk. Running it found three things:

  1.  TAMBON CENTROIDS IGNORED THE PROVINCE. ป่าแดด is a ตำบล in Mueang Chiang
      Mai and an อำเภอ in Chiang Rai. Keyed on the bare name, the Chiang Mai
      centroid answered ต.ป่าแดด อ.ป่าแดด จ.เชียงราย and put a school, a
      hospital and two more 139 KM OUT WHILE DECLARING ±1,365 M. The province
      was known at every call site and was simply not being used. Now keyed
      (province, name), and refused outright when no province is given and the
      name lives in both.
  2.  THAI ROMANISATION DOES NOT AGREE WITH ITSELF ABOUT SPACES. streets.json
      holds Wua Lai and Thapae Road; addresses are written Wualai Rd and Tha
      Phae Rd. Neither pair matched, so shops on two of the busiest roads in
      the old city fell through to a postcode centroid 4 km wide. A second,
      space-blind index — consulted only when the exact one finds nothing,
      minimum key length six — took Ajarn Somphong Tok Sen from ±4,026 m to
      ±806 m. (Tha Phae vs Thapae is a spelling difference, not a spacing one,
      and still misses. A curated alias row is the fix, not a fuzzy matcher.)
  3.  The docstring's accuracy was out of date, and optimistically.
      Re-measured against 3,517 places carrying both an address and a surveyed
      pin:

  Calibration — the stated uncertainty actually covering the truth — is 63%,
  not the 80% the file claims. The street tier's worst case is ถนนโชตนา,
  Highway 107 to Fang: we hold a short in-city fragment and declare ±280 m for
  a road that runs 128 km.

  So published pins are capped at ±3,000 M, which refused 137 of them.

THE BIGGER FIND: MOT DANG HAS NEVER CRAWLED TEMPLES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------


crawl_overpass.py has forty query groups and not one is
amenity=place_of_worship. The 249 surveyed wat pins we held — against 2,691
wat records — arrived sideways, as mueang-map lens points and as neighbours
inside other groups. OSM HAS 1,070 NAMED, PINNED TEMPLES IN CHIANG MAI ALONE.

importers/pin_from_osm.py fetches them (cache: cache/osm-worship/, kept OUT of
cache/overpass/ on purpose — import_all.py folds everything under the latter
into the corpus, and adding a thousand new temple records is a decision about
the catalogue, not a side effect of fixing pins) and matches register records
to surveyed points by name, on unique names only.
A repeated name is settled by the register's own ตำบล/อำเภอ against an addr:
tag, or by the อำเภอ centroid from importers/crawl_admin.py — and otherwise
refused. A wrong pin on a named temple sends someone to the wrong village,
confidently.

crawl_admin.py is new too: OSM boundary relations for both provinces, each
with the radius of its own bounding box. It yields 25 CHIANG MAI AND 18 CHIANG
RAI อำเภอ. Thailand's ตำบล are barely drawn in OSM — level 7 returns เทศบาล
municipalities, not subdistricts — so the district is the real prize, and it
is enough to tell วัดพระบาท in Mae Rim from วัดพระบาท in Chom Thong.

THE HONEST CEILING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------


287 OF 2,116 — 14%. Raising the cap to ±6 km would add ~140 more and make them
worse. The remaining 1,836 are not an algorithm problem:

  -  1,875 REGISTER RECORDS ARE NOT IN OSM UNDER THEIR NAME. Rural temples
     nobody has mapped. Only a survey or a better register moves these.
  -  146 NAMES ANSWER TO MORE THAN ONE PLACE and the register cannot say
     which.
  -  10 OF THE 18 TOK SEN SHOPS HAVE NO ADDRESS AT ALL — the field reads
     "Chiang Mai". Nothing can geocode that; it needs a name-and-street pass.

They stay on pins.html, which is what that page is for, and the assistant now
says "no map pin" rather than inventing a distance.

OPEN, AND NAN'S
------------------------------------------------------------------------------


  1.  FOLD THE 1,070 CM + CR OSM TEMPLES IN AS RECORDS? The cache is sitting
      there. It would roughly double the wat shelf and give every new record a
      surveyed pin — but it is a corpus decision.
  2.  IS ±3 KM THE RIGHT LINE for publishing an approximate pin on a named
      business? ±1.5 km would keep ~30; ±6 km would add ~140.
  3.  A CURATED STREET-ALIAS TABLE (Tha Phae/Thapae, Nimmanhaemin/Nimman
      Haemin, Ratchadamnoen/Rachadamnern…) — a dozen hand-checked rows would
      reach the Latin-script addresses that miss today.


A FOOTGUN, AND ITS FIX (added after it fired)

The problem. geocode_gaps.py defined "the gap" as records marked needs-pin. But
once its own pins have been applied by import_all, those records are marked
approx and drop out of the gap. So a second run measures a smaller problem and
writes a smaller file — and if a capped run and an uncapped run overlap, the
last writer wins and the cap is silently gone. That happened: the file on disk
held 424 pins, including 55 postcode pins at roughly 6 km of error, while the
cap said 286.

The solution. A record already listed in pins.json is still a gap. Both passes
now read the existing file first and re-derive every pin they wrote, under
whatever cap is in force today. Running them twice now gives the same answer as
running them once, which is the only property that makes a number quotable.

The same shape as everything else found today: a measurement that quietly
measured something other than what it claimed.
