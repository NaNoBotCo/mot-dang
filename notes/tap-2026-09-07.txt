3. THE PROTOTYPE

Same card everywhere, and it is a place you can keep pulling on:

    name · reading · what it is · which street · how far and which way
    an open-now lamp ONLY where hours are recorded (absent, never "closed")
    tag chips that FILTER the map — this is finding by tag with no words typed
    the three nearest: tapping one MOVES the card to it, no page load
    open · add to plan · directions
    a tap on bare ground answers too: which street, which ย่าน, three nearest

Three shapes are switchable at the top so you can feel which is right. Drag the
blue dot to move "you" and the distances re-reckon.

My read: sheet on a phone, docked panel on a wide screen, never the centred
modal — the modal covers the thing you just pointed at, and comparing two dots
is the whole gesture.


4. FORKS — answered by you, 9/7 evening (kept here for the record)

  1  SHAPE. Sheet everywhere · sheet on phone + side panel ≥900px · centred modal.
     Cost: the same either way; it is one CSS block. Worst case of the modal: on a
     360-wide phone the card covers the map and a second tap costs a close first.

  2  BARE GROUND. Does a tap on nothing answer "here: this street, these three",
     or stay silent? Cost of answering: nothing to build — /here.html already
     computes it for the reader's GPS; this asks it of any point. Worst case: a
     card opens when you meant to pan, which is why it needs a drag test first.

  3  TAGS. Do the chips FILTER the map, or only link out to the tag page?
     Filtering is what Michael asked for and the API has taken tag= since August.
     Worst case: a tag nobody has answers zero dots — the map goes empty and has
     to say so rather than look broken.

  4  WALKING THE CARD. Tapping a nearby name moves the card in place (no page
     load), or navigates? In place is the exploring verb. Worst case: a reader
     wanders four cards deep and the back button does not undo it — needs the
     card to remember its trail, one more button.

  5  WHICH SURFACES. All 25,889 (soi, events, doi, plan, homepage included), or
     only the browse maps? Cost of all: the same card, mounted in six more places.

  6  THINGS WE DO NOT HOLD. The tiles are full of named buildings and roads that
     are not our records. Tappable — saying "not on our shelves yet" — or not
     tappable at all? This one is a policy call about admitting absence out loud.


5. BUILT — your six answers, in the code (WO-74, evening of 9/7)

New `tapcard.py` → `tap.css` + `tap.js`, offered by page() on the SAME test that
pulls in MapLibre: a page with a map gets the card, including a map built next
year that nobody remembers to wire. md.js keeps its own card underneath for the
phone that never received tap.js, and its three call sites now ask for the card
by name at the moment of the touch — so the place maps, the shelf maps,
/map.html, /here.html, the results map and the toilets page all upgrade without
an edit of their own.

  1  SHAPE — a sheet at the bottom on a phone, a panel docked bottom-right from
     900px, never a centred modal. Nothing is trapped and nothing is dimmed: the
     map stays live under the card, so a reader who opened it by accident is not
     stuck. Every size is in rem and the height is a maximum, so it still works
     at 200% text on a 320px screen; every control is 44px; Escape closes it.
  2  BARE GROUND — silence. A tap that hits no mark opens nothing.
  3  TAGS — the chips filter. Where the map can narrow itself the chip narrows
     it and says how many are left; a tag nobody has says "none on this map"
     rather than quietly showing everything. Where the map cannot (a place map
     draws five dots), the chip opens the same filter on the results map, which
     can, sorted by distance.
  4  WALKING — the three nearest move the card in place, one history entry per
     card. Your phone's own Back walks the trail backwards and then closes it;
     closing hands every entry back so Back does not replay the walk. That is
     the nearest thing to an ADA convention here: the change is on request,
     announced, and reversible with the control the reader already knows.
  5  ALL SURFACES — one head hook covers every map page. The 534 soi maps now
     carry marks that know their own record (they had a <title> and nothing
     else). The results map now hands the card the tags, street, district and
     opening hours the row already held.
  6  THINGS WE DO NOT HOLD — silence. Only our own marks are tappable.

`tests/test_tap.py` runs tap.js under a DOM stub — 26 checks, all green, and
