THE HOLDING PEN — incomplete records, written down plainly
Mot Dang · 2026-09-05


THE PROBLEM

1,246 records in the catalogue hold a name and nothing else.

No coordinate. No phone. No opening hours. No website. No photograph. Most of
them are temples from the government's own temple register, which states a
subdistrict and a district and gives no way at all to find the place.

They are not junk. The register is authoritative and the temples are real. But
a reader who meets one of these has been handed a name and sent away. There are
1,246 of them, which is 5% of the catalogue, and they thin out every shelf and
every search they appear in. A search for a massage in the old city that returns
four shops you can walk to and six names you cannot find is a worse search than
one that returns four.

Deleting them is wrong. They are true, they were expensive to gather, and most
of them are one fact away from being useful. A pin, or a phone number, and the
record works.

So the problem is: how do you keep something that is true, and not yet useful,
without letting it get in the way?


THE SOLUTION

Hold it.

A record is HELD when it has no pin and two facts or fewer. That is the whole
test. The two facts are counted by the ant rank the catalogue already computes —
Thai name, English name, phone, LINE, hours, website, photo, claimed, walked.

A held record keeps everything. It keeps its page, so no link breaks and anyone
looking for it by name still finds it. It keeps its place in the data. It keeps
its provenance.

What it loses is the two places a reader meets the catalogue in bulk: the site
search index, and the assistant's index. It is not offered as an answer, because
it cannot answer anything.

And it lets itself out. The test is recomputed from the record's own fields on
every build. The moment a pin lands, or someone adds a phone number, or a walk
adds opening hours, the record crosses the bar and rejoins — by itself, with
nobody remembering to release it. That is why it is a holding pen and not a bin:
a held record is a record waiting, not a record judged.

The errand list stays where it has always been, on pins.html.


WHAT IS DONE, AND WHAT IS WRITTEN DOWN FOR LATER

Done:
  · build.py grows held(), stamps `held` on every place sidecar, and keeps held
    records out of data/index.json.
  · The assistant's indexer skips them, so they cannot be offered as an answer.

Written down, not built:
  · Shelf pages still list them. The same held() call belongs wherever a
    category page collects its rows; it was not made today because build.py is
    18,000 lines and finding every listing site is an afternoon, not a
    paragraph. One call, several places.
  · pins.html should say the count and the bar out loud — "1,246 records are
    waiting for one fact" — so the errand has a number on it.
  · The bar itself (no pin, rank ≤ 2) is a guess that fits the data we have.
    It should be checked against what readers actually click once there is a
    click to check.

See go/pins for how the pins themselves were filled, and why only 287 of 2,116
could be.
