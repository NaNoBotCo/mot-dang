SEARCH, AS BEER FOUND IT — 2026-09-09
=====================================
Bug report: Pallida (Beer), two phone screenshots, 10:00 today.  Samsung, 5G.
"Search is completely broken since the rebuild."


WHAT HE SAW
-----------
motdang.net/search.html?q=Event and a second shot part-way through typing
"Breakfas".  Both show the heading

    ผลการค้นหา · Search results

with NO NUMBER after it, and underneath a box of shelf chips — ช้าง Elephants,
มวยไทย Muay Thai, เรียนทำอาหารไทย cooking classes, เสริมสวย-ตัดผม barbers,
สุขภาพผู้หญิง women's health.  The same five for both queries.  No result, no
count, nothing that answers the question he asked.

He is not seeing a wrong answer.  He is seeing the page BEFORE the answer, and
on his phone that state did not end.


WHY — THE MEASUREMENT
---------------------
The answer needs data/index.json.  Measured against the live site today:

    data/index.json      49,262,215 bytes raw    9,769,199 on the wire (brotli)
    data/index2.json      3,772,761 bytes raw      608,890 on the wire
    rows                     87,833
    JSON.parse            469 ms — on an M-series Mac, warm cache
    first answer drawn    ~10.6 s — same Mac, wired broadband

The small files land fast: search_shelves.json and search_panels.json in about
0.4 s.  Those draw the shelf chips.  So the chips are what stands on screen for
the whole of the window while index.json downloads and parses, and the heading
sits there with no number.  On a Mac that window is ten and a half seconds.  On
a phone on mobile data it is 9.8 MB to fetch and a 38-million-character string
to parse, and the tab has a memory budget.  Beer's window did not end.

The counts move while you wait.  Reading the same page for "Event" at three
moments gave 2, then 119, then 991.  It does settle — 991, against the API's
997 — but everything before it settles is on screen and looks like an answer.


WHY THE INDEX IS 49 MB — THE `k` FIELD IS 46% OF IT
---------------------------------------------------
Field weights, measured by re-serialising every row of the live file:

    k    21.33 MB   45.8%       t     2.16 MB   4.6%
    n     5.08 MB   10.9%       w     1.74 MB   3.7%
    id    3.96 MB    8.5%       a     1.52 MB   3.3%
    s     2.90 MB    6.2%       r     1.49 MB   3.2%

`k` is the searchable keyword blob.  It has become the place the facet and
provenance BADGE TEXT is written out, in both languages, in full, on every row.
A real row:

    "k": "ข้อมูลยังบาง A thin entry ยังไม่ได้ปักหมุด Not pinned yet
          ยังไม่มีช่องทางติดต่อ No way to reach it held มีหลายแหล่งตรงกัน
          More than one source agrees Condo อ.เมืองเชียงใหม่ ..."

How often each phrase repeats across the 87,833 rows:

    held                              87,799 rows  100.0%
    มีเพจเฟซบุ๊ก Facebook page        68,981 rows   78.5%   2.55 MB (Thai token alone)
    มีเบอร์โทร Phone number           61,181 rows   69.7%   1.90 MB
    ยังไม่มีช่องทางติดต่อ / No way…   14,672 rows   16.7%
    ข้อมูลยังบาง / A thin entry       12,460 rows   14.2%

The tokens that sit on more than half the rows come to 6.6 MB by themselves.

This is not only weight.  Those words are SEARCHABLE, so a facet label competes
with a name.  Searching "Event" during the load window returned lawyers and
accountants carrying the chip "🎪 รับจัดงาน · Event planning" ahead of the two
places actually on the Event Venues shelf.

Every one of these facts is already carried as an int in `t` / `f`, which the
chips render from.  The prose in `k` is a second copy of data the row already
has.

FOUND ON THE WAY — THE FAVICON, ON EVERY PAGE
---------------------------------------------
The emoji sweep replaced the 🐜 inside the favicon's data: URI with an inline
SVG, and that SVG uses double quotes inside a double-quoted attribute:

    rel="icon" href="data:image/svg+xml,<svg ...><text ...><svg class="rowicon" ...

The href ends at `class=`.  The remainder lands in the document, and
document.body.innerText on the live search page begins with a stray `">`.  No
favicon anywhere, and two visible characters at the top of every page.  Checked
on index, search.html and here.html — all three.


WHAT WAS CUT — 2026-09-09, on Nan's instruction
================================================
"strip the badge prose out of k and rebuild. Be brutal and relentless
stripping out extraneous prose EVERYWHERE. And rebuild."

1. tags_layer.py — search_words() no longer writes two kinds of label into `k`:
   * the 9 tags of kind `field` (family `state`, whose own note in
     data/tags.json reads "Describes the entry, not the place")   10.93 MB
   * the mined `attrs.amphoe` and `attrs.tambon` labels, whose words
     build.py already writes into `k` raw a few lines further down  0.49 MB
   Their two siblings in the `area` family STAY: `street` reaches records
   STREET_OF does not, and a postcode is a thing a reader types.
   Nothing routes a query to any muted word — checked search_thesaurus.json,
   search_panels.json and search_landmarks.json for all fourteen strings.
   Every facet stays filterable: `f` and `t` are untouched, and refine.js
   reads those, not the prose.

2. build.py — the RTGS reading is written once, not twice. It went into `a`
   AND under its own key `r`, and on 46,645 of the 46,666 rows carrying both,
   `a` was byte-for-byte `r`.                                       1.16 MB
   The matcher now reads `e.r` beside `e.a`. No row carries `r` without `a`,
   so nothing gains a match it did not have, and the 21 rows whose `a` holds
   a Chinese or Korean name as well keep every word.

3. build.py — data/fortune.json (1.36 MB, 180 days of sky and hexagram) is
   fetched only on a page that draws the widget. It was fetched on EVERY
   page: on search.html, 1.36 MB racing the index for a phone's bandwidth to
   fill an element not in the document. Neither search.html nor the front
   page has ever had it.

4. build.py — data/search_panels.json ships without its `_readme`: 2,795
   bytes of build notes fetched by every reader. Any `_`-prefixed key comes
   off now, so the next note added does not have to be remembered.

PROJECTED, measured against the live index before the build:
    k          20.72 MB -> 7.83 MB   (-62.2%)
    index.json ~46.7 MB -> ~32.7 MB
    and 1.36 MB that search.html no longer fetches at all.

THE RELEVANCE BUG — found 2026-09-09 evening, and it is not the bytes
=====================================================================
Nan: "the only thing you should see if you do a search is results relevant to
that search. Not possible always, but should at least not always be
impossible."

It was impossible for nearly every query of more than one word. Measured live,
index fully loaded, so nothing here is a loading artefact:

    guitar                          9 results   (matches /api/v1 exactly)
    martin guitar                   0
    place to buy a Martin guitar    0

Adding a word that four records carry emptied a result set of nine.

THE ENGINE WAS NEVER WRONG. searchcore, handed the same 87,833-row live index
offline in node, answers:

    guitar                         10 rows, every one a guitar shop
    martin guitar                  35 rows, the guitar shops among them
    buy a martin guitar           489 rows — Guitar House, อินทูน กีตาร์ ชอป
                                   and Intune Guitar Shop ranked FIRST

WO-70's rare-word ranking (Σ log(N/df), added after Michael's report) works.
The answer was correct and then discarded.

WHERE: refine_layer.py, wordsOk(). `st.q` read `as_q` and then FELL BACK to
the plain `q` — what search.html's box sends — and wordsOk demands that EVERY
token appear as a literal lowercase substring of the row. The advanced form's
strictest slot, silently applied to the ordinary search box, AFTER searchcore
had already matched with tiers, coverage and the thesaurus. Not a second
opinion — a veto. One word survived it because an AND of one token is
satisfiable; two words almost never are.

THE FIX: wordsOk now tests `as_q` alone, under its own key `aq`. `st.q` stays
as it was because the .ics download is named from it. search.html sends no
as_ parameter at all — it has no such control — so on the ordinary box
wordsOk returns true and searchcore's ranking stands. advanced.html, which is
where a reader deliberately asks for AND / OR / NOT / exact phrase, is
untouched.

WHY IT SHIPPED: tests/test_refine.py sends `as_q`, `as_oq`, `as_eq` and
`as_epq` — twelve times. It had never once sent `q`. The suite tested the
advanced form and never the box on the page. Two assertions added; against the
old code they fail, and by how much is the size of the bug:

    ?q=นวด คลินิก      old: 2 rows of 88,224      fixed: 88,224
    ?q=martin guitar   old: 0 rows of 88,224      fixed: 88,224

    73 passed, 0 failed
