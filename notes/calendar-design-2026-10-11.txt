Design for Nan, 2026-10-11, after a council of three critics (five rounds, agreed). Baton 0002.

YOUR QUESTION: grid up top, list below — a great reason, or lazy iterative design?
No reason. The list (#soon) was the calendar until the 2026-10-02 A/B put the drawn grid above it, and nothing retired the list.

HER WORDS (2026-10-11)
1) calendar illegible at phone width, non descriptive. 2) items in no particular order; the order should come from something we know about the reader. 3) plan-ahead and near-term events jumbled in one amorphous timeline; long-term calls to action (get a hotel, find a guide) differ from near-term ones (get a Grab, invite a friend). Redundant time data. The doodle clock: not cool, not correct, the same every day; it should change daily and be accurate, informative and interactive.

THE DESIGN

A. The calendar keeps its name, ปฏิทิน · Calendar. Three bands, each with its own layout and actions.

TODAY — from now to the end of today's night (a day ends at 03:00; one boundary for every band, the clock and the gates).
- Rows: start time in large type, the whole title, the place, distance once. On now: "until 16:00". Over: gone.
- Running now (a festival or show on for several days): one line at the top, "on until 13 Oct".
- 5 rows, then "+ 22 more today".
- Actions: Grab there; Invite (the phone's share sheet, and LINE); Map. A tap on the row opens the same card as now: source, map, keep, add to calendar, not for me, hide this kind.

NEXT 14 DAYS — tomorrow to day 14 (her fortnight).
- The index: the grid, kept unless you let it go (fork 1). It starts on today (no crossed-out days) and shows each day's count at 375 px. A tap opens that one day under it (your 10-04 word), as now.
- A day: header (weekday, date, wan phra or festival, the forecast glyph), its events in time order, 5 shown, "+ N more".
- An all-day event sits on its own date only. A festival inside 14 days shows on its days and carries its plan actions with it.
- Actions: Keep; Add to calendar; Invite.

PLAN AHEAD — day 15 to about 7 months.
- Festivals, seasons and long runs as cards ("in 44 days", dates, one line on why to plan, the picture), by date.
- Dated events past day 14 (CMU Chiang Mai Marathon 8 Nov, TEDxChiangMai 14 Nov) as a list by week under the cards.
- Actions: Find a room (fork 4); Add to calendar; the festival's page; Find a guide (fork 3).
- A shared #ev= link to any event opens the band that holds it.

B. Order. Inside every band, time order, so the reader can follow it. What we know about the reader picks which 5 lead each day, each with its reason ("kept", "you look at markets", "1.2 km"). Without history: nearest the city centre with a picture and a time. Fork 2 puts this to you.

C. One state. One filter bar (kinds, Kept, city), one selected day, one router (#ev=, #day=, #when=). The #soon list stays where it is (fork 5) but reads the same data and hands its links to the bands; the #jump chips count from the same data. One city switch for map, forecast and calendar.

D. One time model.
- JS: assets/front/when.js (Bangkok today, the 03:00 day boundary, ymd, span, month and weekday names EN/TH, B.E.); front.js, wx.js and tiles.js read it. It goes into front_layer's copy list and stamp(), and the publish gate test_front_calendar.js moves onto it with every check kept.
- Python: thaitime.py, wrapping importers/thai_lunar.py (held to myhora by its test) and shelflife.today(); front_layer bakes with Bangkok's date instead of the Mac's.
- Data: one bake, front/when.json (events, festivals, wan phra, long runs), ids equal to events.json's k so kept lists and shared links still work. Festival dates from festivals_layer.occurrence() (announced dates, then the rule); festival_calendar.json rows only where it gives none. soon.json and ahead.json stop being baked once nothing reads them. front.js falls back to events.json while when.json is absent.
- The other date code copies (about 40 month tables, 70 B.E. sums, in about 30 files) stay out of this plan; a sweep can come to you later with its cost.

E. The clock, later slice.
- Colour on the rim by Thai weekday (Sunday red, Monday yellow, Tuesday pink, Wednesday green, Thursday orange, Friday blue, Saturday purple); the face stays light, the hands black (4.7:1 or better on all seven).
- Tonight's moon in a window; a lotus on wan phra; the festival's emblem on a festival day.
- Under the doodle, the hour in Thai beside English: บ่ายสองโมง · bai song mong · 2 in the afternoon.
- Stays: gears and the Thai date pop (B.E., C.S., Lanna month), the ant, rain drops, sun and moon, turning the dial, tapping a tag.

HOW IT SHIPS
- The new calendar shows only under ?cal=next until your word. The commit that drops the flag is the go-live, and the A/B's count restarts that day.
- Each slice carries a check that fails on today's code first: when.js over the baked file (one band per event, all-day at 00:00, the 03:00 boundary, the phone's time zone), and a 375 px headless-Chrome pass on the Mac whose screenshots come to you.
- Slices: 1 the time model and when.json; 2 the three bands under the flag; 3 order and reasons; 4 actions; 5 the clock's colour, moon and hour word (live, no flag: nothing removed).

FORKS FOR NAN (if silent by 2026-10-18, the default holds)
1. Removals. The grid, the doodle's 1/9 pager (its events move to TODAY) and the #ahead ribbon (its festivals move to PLAN AHEAD). Each: keep, or let go. Default: keep each. Worst case of keeping: two ways into the same days stay on the page.
2. Order. (a) Time order, the reader's fit picks the 5 shown per day, a reason on each. (b) Rows ordered by fit, a reason on each, times out of order. (c) Time order, all shown, a "for you" mark. Default (a). Worst case of (a): a reader with no history sees the nearest 5, not their 5. Worst case of (b): the complaint you made today, explained.
3. Find a guide. No guide directory is on this disk. (a) A link to the Tourism Department's register of licensed guides, $0. (b) motdang.net Co.'s helpers (Beer's per-job people): a price per job, someone to answer. (c) No guide button. Default (c). Worst case of (c): your ask is not built. Worst case of (b): a reader books and nobody answers.
4. Find a room. (a) The map opened on motdang's own hotel layer around the festival (5,875 in Chiang Mai, 2,238 in Chiang Rai), $0. (b) A paid booking link (affiliate_layer). Default (a). Worst case of (a): a map of places to stay that books none.
5. The #soon list. (a) Retire it once you have seen the new calendar live. (b) Keep it. No default; it stays until you say.
6. The A/B. (a) The new calendar becomes the calendar arm; the count restarts at go-live. (b) The calendar sits under the stage for everyone, the stage shows the map only, the A/B ends. Default (a).
