MOT DANG — ONE CALENDAR, ONE CLOCK, ONE TIME MODEL
Design for Nan, 2026-10-11. Baton 0002. Nothing here is built yet.

HER WORDS (2026-10-11)
1) calendar illegible at phone width, non descriptive. 2) items in no particular order; the order should come from something we know about the reader. 3) plan-ahead and near-term events jumbled in one amorphous timeline; long-term calls to action (get a hotel, find a guide) differ from near-term ones (get a Grab, invite a friend). Redundant time data. The doodle clock: not cool, not correct, the same every day; it should change daily and be accurate, informative and interactive. Grid up top and list below on the front: lazy iterative design.

WHAT IS THERE NOW (preview of today's code, 375 px, 09:38 Sunday)
- Grid: 7 columns of 45 px cells holding a day number and a picture. No titles, no counts. The first row is six crossed-out past days.
- Day list under it: 15:00, 14:00–16:00, 19:30, all day, 17:00, 08:30. The sort is a score the reader cannot see. Distance prints twice on a row ("· ~2.1 km" and "~2.1 km away").
- The clock: a 24-hour dial, midnight at the foot. At 09:38 the hour hand sits where a wall clock says 11. Tags for 06:30 and 08:30 events float on it after they have ended. Same look every day.
- Events are listed in five places on the front: the clock's tags, the hero pager ("Today 12:30 · 1/9"), the fortnight list, the old #soon list, the #jump chips. Each computes "today" its own way. #soon drops an all-day event two hours after it starts; the fortnight keeps it to midnight. #big counts days in UTC; the ribbon counts in Bangkok time.
- Festivals reach the front through two bakes of one file (soon.json, capped at 4, and ahead.json), each with its own date resolver; festivals.html has two more. Festival dates are kept in four files.
- Date helpers: Thai month names defined about 40 times in about 30 files; Buddhist Era +543 written about 70 times; span() 4 times; the week-minute "open now" sum 3 times. front_layer bakes with the Mac's date, not Bangkok's.
Full audit: this file's companion, notes/calendar-audit-2026-10-11.txt.

THE DESIGN

A. One calendar, three horizons
The fortnight grid, its day list, the #ahead ribbon and the hero pager become one section, "When" (เมื่อไร). Three bands, each its own layout and its own actions:

NOW — on now and later today, until 03:00.
- Layout: a list, one row per event, time first in large type, the whole title, the place, minutes away.
- Order: what you can still make, soonest first. On now: "until 16:00". Starting: "in 40 min". An event already over leaves the band.
- Actions on the row: Grab there; Invite (the phone's share sheet, LINE as its own button); Map.

THIS WEEK — tomorrow to day 7.
- Layout: a strip of 7 day chips across the top (weekday, date, count of events). The chip is the index; tapping it scrolls the list to that day. Under the strip, one block per day: a day header (weekday, date, wan phra or festival if any, the forecast in one glyph), then up to 5 rows, then "+ 12 more".
- Order inside a day: by start time. The reader decides which 5 lead (see C).
- Actions on the row: Keep; Add to calendar; Invite.

PLAN AHEAD — day 8 to about 7 months.
- Layout: cards, one per festival, season or long run (an exhibition, a run of shows), with "in 44 days", the dates, one line on why to plan, and the picture.
- Order: by date.
- Actions on the card: Find a room (flies the map to the festival and switches on the map's own stay layer, the hotels motdang already lists); Add to calendar; the festival's page; Find a guide (a fork, below).

The grid goes. The week strip is the index for THIS WEEK; PLAN AHEAD needs none. A "Fortnight" or "Month" view can come back later as an index of the same list if she asks.

B. Legible at 375 px
Every row carries the whole title (wraps, never cut), the time, the place, minutes away. Distance prints once. No 45 px cells. The week strip scrolls sideways if 7 chips do not fit. Tested at 375 and 1280 by the gate.

C. Order from what we know about the reader
Signals the page already holds: where they are (md-here, 20 minutes); the time now; the city they picked (one switch, shared by map, forecast and calendar instead of three); what they open, keep and hide (md-cal); language.
- NOW orders by time the reader can arrive: minutes to start, less travel time from md-here. Without a location it orders by start time.
- THIS WEEK: each day shows the 5 that fit the reader best, in time order, each with its reason in one chip ("you keep markets", "1.2 km", "kept"). Without history the 5 are the nearest to the city centre with a picture and a time. "+ N more" opens the rest, in time order.
- The score stays one function, read by every band; it no longer writes into the shared event objects.

D. One state
One filter bar (Kind chips, Kept, city), one selected day, one URL form: #when=2026-10-14 and #ev=<id> both open the calendar band that holds them. Old #day= links keep working by redirect to the same place.

E. One time model
- Python: one module, thaitime.py (Bangkok today, B.E., Thai and Lanna months, weekday colours, wan phra, a festival's dates from festivals.json). front_layer, festivals_layer and answers_layer move onto it first; the rest of the 30 files move in later slices.
- JS: one module, assets/front/when.js (bkk(), ymd, span(), month and weekday names EN/TH, B.E.). front.js, wx.js and tiles.js read it.
- Data: front_layer bakes one front/when.json — events, festivals, wan phra and moon days, long runs — dated in Bangkok time, each item {id, kind, start, end, allDay, city, ...}. The old events.json, soon.json and ahead.json stay baked until nothing reads them.

F. The clock (about 20% of the doodle, as she set on 2026-10-02)
- Correct at a glance: a 12-hour face with hour and minute hands. Around it a thin 24-hour ring shades night and day from today's sunrise and sunset.
- Thai time under the face, the word for the hour: ตีสาม, หกโมงเช้า, เที่ยง, บ่ายสองโมง, สี่โมงเย็น, สองทุ่ม. A tap on the word says it in English ("2 in the afternoon").
- Changes daily: the face takes the Thai colour of the weekday (Sunday red, Monday yellow, Tuesday pink, Wednesday green, Thursday orange, Friday blue, Saturday purple). The moon in a window on the face shows tonight's phase; on wan phra a small lotus; on a festival day that festival's emblem.
- Informative: marks on the ring for events still ahead today (at most 5, gone once they end); sunrise and sunset ticks.
- Interactive: drag the hour hand to travel the day; the NOW band and the map follow (calAt, mapAt, as today). Tap a mark to open that event.

G. #soon, the old list
The new calendar does everything #soon does. Retiring #soon removes a section readers can reach: her word. Until then it stays, unchanged.

SLICES (each behind the preview, each a commit, each through the gates)

2. The NOW, THIS WEEK and PLAN AHEAD bands in place of #month, #mday, #ahead and the hero pager; one filter state; legible at 375; the gate extended.
